# The De-Prostitution Hypothesis

To reconstruct 2nd century cultural literature context of Roman satire in Early Christian texts,
it is useful to consider a hypothesis that Earth Christian sects start with schools for women who specialize in educating previous prostitutes.

In the ancient Roman empire during the 1st and 2nd century, it was common for men, specially those of higher status,
to have sexual encounters with lots of women during their life.

The word "concubine" was not negatively loaded in context of the Roman elite.
On the contrary, to be a concubine was often one of the few ways ordinary women could ascend in social status.
A primary reason for this, is that concubines have physical access to people of power.
A concubine might overhear some state secret or conspiracy, appeal to a person of power on behalf of some group
and even poison their lover, if they were persuaded by others by political ideology, bribes or blackmail.

A frequent method to demonstrate political power among men of high status, was to conquer concubines of their rivals.
This was particularly humiliating for the rival if the lover was betrothed or a wife
and when the rival underwent circumcision in adult age in order to satisfy religious requirements for marriage.
For example, the marriage between Felix and Drusilla in Acts of the Apostles was a great political scandal for Drusilla's earlier husband.
Drusilla was also daughter of Herod Agrippa I, which added to the humiliation since her bloodline was royal.

The way Paul acts in Acts, is as if Felix and Drusilla were behaving immorally.
However, this could be Roman satire.
In general, people where laughing of the person who became humiliated,
so a lesson of morality might be interpreted as a joke,
adding to the humiliation, something that would never be done to the face of Felix and Drusilla,
but aimed at the audience who this texts was written for, to their indulgence.
The complexity of a such joke increases with the fact that Drusilla and her only child died under the eruption of Mount Vesuvius.
One can think about it as a humiliation so strong, that it was punished by the gods.
This appealed to the tragedy aspects of ancient comedy, where theatre plays full of jokes often ended badly for the main characters.
The Paul character disrupts the realism of the play, by prophesying about the coming doom in a subtle way,
which supports the argument that Early Christian authors were experimenting with meta-literature,
a common elite prestige sign of Roman satire.

In addition to frequent roles as concubines, women also engaged in religious prostitution.
Religious prostitution is part of ancient religions, practiced by men, often as eunuchs, and women.
Therefore, having many sexual relationships in cities was not uncommon.
This was not something to be embarrassed about, in most sects.
A few sects were opposed to it and thought about such sexual relationships as impure activities and sinful,
in large part because of the political role concubines played.
By these few sects, concubines and women in general were seen as of lower status and associated with corruption.
This made it even more fun for Roman poets, to produce Roman satire where they used a main character from a such sect,
who were shocked by all this "sinful" activity.
On top of this, using a savior figure of some mystery cult, that grants forgiveness of sins by a simple prayer,
adds to the view that, having many sexual relationships, was something that excited their audience.
Roman poets do not write such texts to teach people moral lessons,
but they also add tragic elements to bypass censorship.
The impending doom is an escape from the thought police, where smut literature gets passed off as religious teachings.

Early Christians write about adultery, boys sneaking into women's baths, incest and rape.
Virgin births also serve as a kind of joke, that is taught to children, to the amusement of adults.
Now, a teacher of righteousness, who belongs to a sect from a remote region of the empire,
that traditionally punishes adultery with death for women,
while making exceptions all the time for them and hanging around prostitutes,
is not something that would be historically plausible.
This whole literary concept is about drama and sex, things people would like to read,
but written in a such way to avoid suspicion from religious authorities.
One can think about many Early Christians as perverts, that use their religion to explore sexuality,
which is supported by the many sexual rituals that were common among these sects.
Their opposition to official religions, might not be a critique of religious prostitution,
but an attempt to take ownership of their own sexuality, as perverted individuals.
Whether authors debate about virginity or sexual relationships,
the key point is personal ownership of sexuality.
Loyalty to some sect, is kind of a declaration of sexual independence and protection against unwanted forms of social control.

Most scolars believe today that most Early Christians were women.
Since 1/3 of women died in childbirth, it would mean that many of these women were relatively young.
In addition, only 1 in 4 adults were women on average.
By using statistics alone, one can argue that many of these women must have multiple sexual relationships.

Girl babies were less desirable than boy babies, so they were more often put out on the street,
to be picked up by somebody who might either adopt them, for example by not being able to have their own children,
or groomed into prostitution for profit.
Prostitution is a way for girls to survive, when they are unwanted by their own parents.
A lot of cults would be controlled by charismatic leaders, who used their female followers for sex,
often at a young age, where official doctrine was merely a cover for activities that today would be criminal.

One can also think about the mechanism of religious conversion in this context.
Most ancient religions were family traditions, designed to protect the social structure of their family relationships.
This was needed to gain high status in society.
Marriages were often political and strategic, often arranged based on the idea of protecting and increasing the wealth of a family.
Wedding gifts were often a very important income for such families.
The higher social status of a woman, the more precious wedding gifts.
This means, when a woman converts from their family religion to a cult,
it could be because she is exiled from her own family and needs to survive by selling her own body.
To survive could be as simple, as the cult demanding sex for food.
Such women become sex slaves.

The scandal of cults, is that a women is brainwashed into following a set of teachings that makes them vulnerable to sexual exploitation.
Again, this is a humiliation, because the woman's sexuality is seen as property by men, something to be conquered.
It is a humiliation for a family to lose a female family member to a cult.

When one considers the higher amount of Early Christian followers that were women,
a very complex social dynamics emerges where cults are both exploiting these women sexually,
but also defending themselves from accusations, that they are stealing women from families to use them as sex slaves,
since this can lead to persecution and trials, often under the name of attempting to start new religions based on lies.
Over time, as such cults grow and the cult leaders age, with followers having their own children,
the sexual relationships are traded off with social status by establishing family structures within the cults.

With other words, most likely, the first Early Christians cults were human trafficking rings, that exploited young girls sexually.
As the criminal leaders of these rings were accused of piracy, which is illegal human trafficking,
not necessarily about human trafficking in general,
they make up a story about their own savior figure, being falsely accused of piracy and crucified.
This story is written as Roman satire, to leverage cognitive biases of misogyny, in attempt to avoid persecution as pirates.
Over time, the pirates accumulate wealth and seek to gain higher social status, by mimicking elite literature.
Since the value of sexual relationships goes down over time in relation to being acknowledged and accepted in society,
the criminal leaders participate in social programs to educate their own followers, who were mostly women.

This leads to The De-Prostitution Hypothesis, which can be summarized as following:

1. The first Early Christian cults starts with human trafficking, which would include eunuchs and young girls.
2. These cults are accused of illegal human trafficking, or piracy.
3. To be accepted socially and gain higher social status, these cults start educating their own followers.

Eunuchs were used in governments, serving in roles of high importance, because they were easier to control.
This again got to do with the role of concubines, that were used to leverage physical access for political purposes.
Under religious prostitution, eunuchs were often bisexual, having sexual relationship with both men and women.
However, in government functions, eunuchs were often asexual, to avoid all the problems with concubines.
Those eunuchs that were educated in Early Christian cults to gain higher social status,
took vows of celibacy, which enabled them to be used in government functions.
Therefore, the leaders of the cults that de-prostituted their own followers, became priests who took vows of celibacy,
both to signal the future directions of these cults and to serve as a moral example to their own flock.
It is not necessarily that the priests stopped having sexual relationships,
but that their official roles where asexual to impress the government.

The evidence here is simply that there were lots of women among Early Christians,
and the lack of historical records of any names of ordinary poor Early Christians from the entire 2nd century.
The absence of such historical records, suggests that the nature of these sexual relationships were wished to remain secret.
Since Early Christian cults did not gain a large following, they must have a lower social status.
This increases the probability of sexual exploitation of women, from what we know about ancient Roman culture in general.
However, in this mechanism of human trafficking and sexual exploitation,
there was a pathway forward compatible with gaining higher status and education.
This is also consistent with the evidence from the 2nd century of Early Christianity.

Persecution of Early Christians by Roman authorities, can be explained by the past history of the cult leaders as pirates.
In history, persecution often continues for centuries, without any of those who persecute, having a clear reason to do it.
Thus, after the process of de-prostitution, the persecution continued and causing problems for people in government functions.
At the end of the 2nd century, Marcia, the concubine of Commodus, appeals to the emperor on behalf of Early Christian prisoners.
This event is like a historical keyhole, connecting the role of concubines to Early Christians and possible function in government.
Otherwise, it is unlikely that somebody would have enough political influence to use the concubine of the emperor.

The De-Prostitution Hypothesis is compatible with The Ouroboros Hypothesis,

This text was used on liturgical basis, which means it took a year to get through it.

Where the original mocks translation fables in Hellenistic culture, which is Roman satire reacting to persecution and accusation,
