# Wells and Getting Water

To reconstruct 2nd century cultural literature context of Roman satire in Early Christian texts,
it is useful to understand the complex social world and politics around wells and getting water.

In the ancient world, water could be a scarce resource, but it also could be in abundance.
Water shaped the mental and cognitive landscape in addition to being a vital resource.
It is surprising how different this mental landscape is from the modern mind in developed countries,
where access to water is usually simply turning on a tap or drinking from a bottle.

### 1. The basic physical features of a well

Most people today do not stop to think carefully about the basic physical features of a well.
This is because we live in a world were water pumps and standardized energy infrastructure is taken for granted.
So, when reading texts from the ancient world,
we tend to gloss over details in language that carried significant meaning at the time.

First, it is important to realize that wells are not primarily about fun and excitement.
To the extent that people associated it with fun and excitement,
is due to women using wells as a social space for sharing rumors,
taking turns to look out for kids playing nearby and to empathize with each other around their common curse: The well.

The well above all other literature genres represents tragedy, due to obvious reasons.
A well itself is a symbol, a machine, stating an obvious fact, that accessing water is difficult.
With easy access to water there would be no reason to build a well.
Every aspect about wells invoked memories of hard labor and pain,
which added to any psychological trauma related to conflicts around wells.

However, the real reason the well is closely associated with tragedy in ancient literature,
is because as much it represents hardship, it is a symbol of "this is as good as it is ever going to get".
A well changes the meaning of the surrounding empty landscape from potentially bountiful and fertility of nature,
to barren and dry, or even worse when there are no people: Death or a wasteland hostile to human life.

When people say "a deep well" about a person today, they can mean that the person have deep thoughts full of meaning.
However, in the ancient world, a deep well means more manual labor to bring the water to the surface.
The best way to understand and appreciate this fact is to do the math.

Physically, the potential energy in a local gravitational field is:

     E = m * g * h

     E = energy
     m = mass
     g = local gravitational constant
     h = height

This means, the minimum work required to bring water from the ground to the surface,
is in a straight line upwards, using a bucket as light as possible.

To minimize the work required to lift the bucket, it is cheaper for the person to stand at the surface applying forces,
while using a rope attached to the bucket over the edge, or using a device, often made of wood, to minimize friction against the rope.

Any extra labor applied to bringing water to the surface, beyond a minimum smooth vertical movement, was a waste of energy.
This extra energy meant that one had to work harder to grow more food, which again required more water.
So, wasted energy carried rents: The waste propagated as metaphorical waves of negative consequences into the future.

Yet, with a simple cut into the rope using a knife, sword or a sharp rock,
an adversary could bring destruction to society as a whole with minimal effort.
This attack could be done in a brief moment or in stages where the rope was partially damaged
and eventually would snap some time in the future when the event was impossible to trace back to the culprit.

This meant that people had to keep extra buckets and extra rope as a backup, in case of an emergency.
If they were down to the last bucket and rope, then they had to keep watch over the well.
This required armed guards that would kill anyone that posed a threat.
When it came to this moment, they were not kidding around, but took this duty dead seriously,
because all the lives of their entire society depended on it.

To bring up lost buckets and damaged rope from the well that could block access to water, a person had to go down there.
This required equipment and collaboration.
For smaller fixes one might use hooks attached to ropes to fish out the bucket and rope.

Sometimes, a person or animal might fall into the well.
An enemy might e.g. drop a lamb into the well that would drown and poisen the water with its corpse.
Blood from a person or animal could also threaten the water supply.

The point is that in the base case scenario, the community had to supply enough energy to bring the water to the surface,
but in every other possible scenario the default was something much, much more horrible.
Furthermore, many of these possible scenarios could happen with much less energy than bringing the water to the surface.
This was why the well above all other literature genres represented tragedy.

### 2. Pottery as a means to transport water

Not only was the minimum energy required around wells an absolute constraint on physics,
where any deviation meant wasting energy that carried rents into the future,
but once the water got to the surface, it had to be brought from the well to the required destination.

To transport water one required a container and the most common way to produce a such container was pottery.
A water pot was often carried on top of the head.
Since the water pot was carried this way, the better posture
the less energy was used and the less harm it did to long term health.

The person who carried water had to be in good physical shape and due to the posture it was easy to tell
at distance whether the person was physically attractive.
So, when somebody writes in the ancient world about a person carrying water,
it could be a literary device to tell something about the posture and body build of the person,
that might invoke certain emotions of desire in people.
The reason authors write this way is to let people use their imagination, while achieving more with less.
It is not only about the physical shape and function, but also extending to the role of the person in society.
Carrying water is something that makes the whole society go around,
beyond the basic physical features of the well, which had positive meaning, although the person could have low status.

Remember that any deviation from the minimum energy of getting water was a waste.
If the person that carried a water pot on their head stumbled, then the result could be not only wasted water,
but they had to go back to the well to fetch more.
This required extra labor which was added to the daily burden that took more energy and time that also delayed other activities.
Waste in this sense is not only loss in materialistic wealth, but also in the long term.
In the worst case when stumbling, the pottery might break and require replacement.

In psychology there is something called "the fundamental attribution error".
As a standard example to explain this phenomena, one uses a person A that stumbles on a rock.
A person B observing A when stumbling, might think "What an idiot who don't see the rock",
while A might think "What an idiot that placed this rock in the way of people walking".
What matters is that A and B have completely different perspectives of interpreting what is happening and what needs fixing.
Due to the phenomena of fundamental attribution error,
it is basically impossible in the ancient world to create a communal sense of mutual understanding around the activity of carrying water.

When a person stumbles while carrying water, is matters greatly whether this was observed or not.
Anyone can make mistakes, but whether this mistake was made in public or private is the difference between shame or a small correction.
Now, put this together with the symbolism of pottery, that could easily break.
Making an error in public (metaphorically: to stumble), could mean that there is shame attached to it.
Pottery is not just a device for transporting water, but a symbol for destruction by deviating from the norm of social behavior.
What gives pottery this specific meaning in literature is the well, that is both a curse on society in terms of constraints of energy,
but also the metaphorical cornerstone to make things go around smoothly overall.
The well gives meaning to pottery in a relationship that was associated with the relationship between a master and a slave.
Hence, while bringing water can be a positive term, it can also be used as a threat,
a way to demand obedience by the one that commands it, that links slavery with being shamed in public.
It means that if you are not obedient, then more shame in public will be followed.
Pottery in this sense can mean a threat that carries more hidden and unspeakable threats with it recursively,
that overall would mean vulnerable and fragile in relation to some higher power.

Pottery that contains water is the basic building block of symbolic usefulness in the literature of the ancient world.
Now, to understand the miracle performed by Jesus or Dionysus of turning water into wine,
it is not as much about the substance of water versus wine (still an important materialistic point),
but spiritually the meaning is that this transformation of water into wine, happens while the water is inside the pot.
With other words, it is an expression of the person who has a useful function in society,
transforming by being initiated into the mystery cult into having a greater role that transcends usefulness.
It is a symbolic language that both keeps the person under control, because the wine is still within the pot,
but also giving hints of surplus energy and spiritual enlightenment.
In addition to this personal sublime message, it is a self-referential language where Jesus or Dionysus does it with multiple pots filled with water,
converting new people to their mystery religion.
It is like, by telling this story the audience is witnessing their own transformation and also becoming aware of the surrounding audience,
thinking about the same way, sharing this moment of greatness and awesomeness in their activity of worship.

First, you have to be a useful member of society, which means to bring in money to the priests.
Second, the money you bring in to the priests can never guarantee salvation,
because salvation can only be achieved by transcending usefulness,
that is both a promise to the person seeking salvation and demand at the same time.
The purpose is to keep people in a state of confusion where the interpretation depends on the mood of the person,
so they can be easily controlled and steered in the direction where the priest wants them to be socially and politically.

In short, metaphorically speaking, people bring water to the priests (money or gifts) and in return they get wine,
a symbol of the intoxicated mental state of ecstasy, that is imagined to transfigure the water in place.
This means, the priests are pretending that the money they get from you is never leaving your pocket,
but transforming in place into emptiness that you will be happy to discover when realizing that the money are not worth anything, in your possession.
No, the priests never receive the actual money from you, it just seem to vanish in thin air (which is how the priests spend the money).
This is why the priests always need more money.

It is pretty clever, is it not? Pottery in this sense is not just a container of usefulness or wealth,
but a symbol that negates the very autonomy of the person that carries pottery on the head with great posture and attractiveness.

"Pottery, keep bringing that water for me. I'll turn it into wine for you."

### 3. Women controlled access to water in dry regions - due to complex cognitive requirements

Keeping water usage under control in dry regions was a very cognitive demanding task,
that had to keep up to date with everyday events at the same time steering the planning process according to long term goals.
If a society had surplus of water, then they could afford to become more ambitious.
However, if a society lacked water, then they would have to scale down their ambitions and ensure their investments were safe.

It is not only a matter of bringing water to the surface, but also keeping track of where the water is spent in society.
No matter how much work is put into bringing water helps, if society just wastes it away.
So, somebody had to be in control of water usage such that those who wasted it were punished and those who saved it were rewarded.

Imagine that you want to build a building.
For example, you could be a priest that wants the community to extend the temple with a pleasant apartment for your personal enjoyment,
since the new story you invented partially based on another deity in the neighbor village,
made people you command under yourself more than usual obedient and easily fooled.

The amount of energy put into construction is higher than the average energy usage,
which also requires more equipment and materials that needs to be made by somebody,
that in turn need to eat more food that has to be grown on the fields,
which might not handle this load without supplying the field with extra water.

Maybe the people in the temple is more than usual obedient and easily fooled,
but the people at the well bringing water to the surface and carrying it around know that this idea will cost them greatly.
They have to do this on top of the extra daily burden and all the other ambitious projects of other people,
(including your own previous incomplete ambitious projects that turned out to be bad ideas), they are currently wasting energy on.
Furthermore, as a priest you are busy trying to scam people out of their money when an opportunity is present,
which means that your mind is occupied watching out for those moments when, metaphorically speaking,
some sheep are not aware it is their turn to be slaughtered.

The priests and priestesses in the ancient world are often involved in plotting and conspiracies to enrich themselves in rare events
that might occur just a handful of times during the span of their whole lives.
They are busy focusing on these important events,
in addition to tracking calendars for sowing and harvesting,
star observation in the night to look for divine intervention (including obsessing over the path of Venus),
plus examining the organs of slaughtered animals for signs of the will of deities.
In their superior intellect and ability to trick people to do their will,
do you think they have time to worry about something as mundane as water usage?

Any actual work done in the ancient world to make society function was performed by women and slaves.
As a thank for this lifelong commitment and continuous struggle,
basically no one was ever mentioned by the people who had one job: To write history down.
Just think about this for a moment, that we do not have any name of a single ordinary poor Christian from the 2nd century.
It is not just undermining the idea that oral tradition could go back to the 1st century in reliable history,
which is a modern fairy tale produced by scholars that do not want to admit people got stuff from oracles (usually intoxicated women babbling something),
but also the fact that it was simply not a priority to record any actual events of historical interest among Early Christian authors.
To reconstruct how the complex social and political environment worked around wells and bringing water,
we have to derive it from physics and archeology, because the sources we have, despite heavily using language influenced by this activity,
show no interest to put down the basic stuff on paper.

So, who do you think was put into charge of handling water usage?

Let me break it down and do it simple for you, because you probably have never thought through it
(like most people living in developed countries never stop to think about how difficult water access was in the ancient world):

Start at the bottom of society and work your way up until you find somebody you can trust with this task.

1. Slaves? No, you can not trust slaves with something where an error might kill the whole society you are living in.
2. Women? Hmm... yes! Better make sure they never think highly of themselves, though.

This means, what can be easily predicted from this model:

1. Women controlled access to water in dry regions
2. Women played side characters and subordinate roles to men in stories when being around wells

Wait a minute! Did you actually believe that Jesus meeting a woman by the well in the gospel of John carried
actual divine revelation instead of just being another play on very specific human conditions in the ancient world turning into spiritual metaphors?

If you are feeling annoyed about this condescending way of talking,
then try think about how the women felt about the well in the ancient world.
Yeah, you are getting it.

The cognitive load to handle water usage was very demanding,
which had to be performed at some minimum base level,
just like there was a minimum amount of energy to bring the water to the surface.
Any deviation from this base cognitive load was a waste,
so worrying about it or trying to figure out another way around it,
just added to the burden instead of releaving it.
There simply was no magic way out, not when the highest intellects at the time are still are playing around with primes and epicycles.
It would take over another thousand years before people thought of turned minimizing pain and maximizing pleasure into a discipline: Science.

What? Did you think science was just an oppressive scheme to rob people of their political freedom?
Maybe you should spend some more time thinking about what science was in the beginning and how it got started.

Just like people are complete idiots that waste energy today (wink, wink),
guess what women at the well felt when men decided in their wisdom to spend the water on... yet another war.
Which they did, over and over for centuries.
Women were watching men taking their precious energy and demanding more and more. And more.

Imagine feeling this, over and over and yet knowing that when you go to sleep,
there is nothing you can do about it and you have to stop thinking about it, even how absurd it is,
because when you wake up you are forced to DO THE SAME THING over and over to keep everything
from falling apart and you causing the end of your own life and
everyone you loved and there is no person in power you can talk to about it.

No wonder that many women in the ancient world simply went insane.
As a result, women got a reputation among men for being emotionally vulnerable.
Infuriating, is it not? This is what "the fundamental attribution error" means historically.

However, what women around the well had, in the least, were each other.
Since the only barrier against insanity were other women like themselves,
you can imagine how welcome other people were nearby the well.
To be blunt, other people were not welcome at all.

Yet, at the top of bringing water, women were also responsible for raising kids until they separated into social roles.
The economics of this is simple: The smallest amount of women and effort required to look out for kids,
while also serving as a temporary pause against the hard labor of bringing water the surface,
is by having large amount of kids guarded by a few women nearby the well, taking turns.
This means they do not have to walk far from the well to the place where the kids are and back.
Yet, the kids can not be too close to the track where people are walking with water pots on their heads.
Women had to keep track of the kids and be prepared to step in when they got into fights,
while also trying to spend as little energy as possible worrying about stuff,
yet they had to worry above the base minimum to keep the water usage functioning in a modest way.

To make this work, you had to get rid of all distractions, which means: Get rid of all other people.
Other people, just had to, to keep women from becoming insane, to simply get out of the way.
They could not cause a disturbance and not distract,
or they would be hated by all the women in the village,
who had access to how the food is made and what people drink.
Any person being a distraction was a danger that the village could not afford in terms of water usage.

Yet, some people are born to cause trouble.

### 4. The basic problem of abusive behavior and how it gives rise to power dynamics

The human brain has not evolved to handle the complex social and political environment around wells in the ancient world.
This means, the tool we use for reasoning is not the proper one for the job.
While hunter and gatherer societies were complex, they were not huge, but usually contained around 50 people.
This is the level of complexity that the human brain excels at.
If there are fewer people around, then it gets boring.
If there are more people around, the human brain can not keep up.

Some people are born with reduced capacity to respond to negative stimuli.
This means, they do not learn to fit in properly socially and fail to receive lessons from people around them
to internalize the social dynamics that help most people to survive and stay healthy.
When in addition these people experience cognitive overload,
they can develop social insecurity and act in aggression or malevolence toward others.

The need to belong in society is a basic psychological need that when not satisfied,
can make people urge for more control and more resources.
However, since they already live in a complex world that is too much for them,
there is never a stop signal that gives them rest, as the brain produces
over-simplified narratives about the solution being just around the corner.
These people continue to urge for more and more, until they die.

So, when you combine this phenomena with the complexity of wells and getting water,
you get disruptive events that causes the power structure to flip upside down and back.

Sometimes, the women by the well are in control, after the priests and priestesses have caused too much trouble
and society stops listening to the lies they are being told.
Society in these times turns back, closer to a minimum energy usage with less ambitions,
that lets the women by the well breathe more and catch up.
Over time, the surplus energy generated in this period allows more ambitions and more social complexity.
This usually builds up to a climax where some disruptive event again brings society back to the hands of women.

On a large scale, people are used to think about the cycles of complexity in whole civilizations.
However, the same thing happens in individual villages over the ancient world in over time in shorter cycles.
Over time, religious texts are altered to include more and more abusive language toward women,
because abusive behavior is the character trait that is typical for the people that ascend to power in times of surplus energy.
They continue being more abusive until they sabotage the basic physical constraints to keep society functioning,
when people die in larger numbers than usual and the effort required can not sustain the number of people living.

The basic problem of abusive behavior is that if the women by the well can not function mentally or physically,
this kills off people and animals until the energy equilibrium is reached.
This happens automatically, because there is a hard constraint on energy usage that is minimum.
You only need somebody with abusive behavior enough to make a such reset, in order for a such cycle to happen.
In societies with shorter cycles there is stronger tendency of abusive behavior.
Very rarely, there are other causes of a reset, such as women making an error in judgement,
because their culture is about minimizing the risk for such events.
Abusive behavior is the most common cause of the reset,
simply because it is outside of control of the women by the well.

The basic power dynamics here is that one thing causes creation and another thing causes destruction.
Without access to water, there can be no human life.
Yet, there is no well defined sense that determines how a society is supposed to function.
People are searching for the answer, unfortunately the only way they know is by using spiritual language.
The priest and the priestesses' job is to look for this answer, but they are often the very ones causing destruction.
A period of over-zealous followers can cause an unnecessary war that invariably hurts those who support society the most.
This is because in war, the enemy always attack the most vulnerable ones which death or mentall illness causes most damage.

A priest or priestess does not think about the well being of the community they live in,
as a different thing than their own self-interest.
They understand that a wealthy community means more prosperity and opportunities to scam people for themselves,
but the community as an isolated entity has no value to them.
There is none or little interest in celebrating the common man or woman or to record their lives for the sake of history.
What the priest or priestess struggle for, is designing stories that they can use to get a little more,
to fill in the empty hole after feeling not properly belonging in society.
They commit fraud because... they feel being not worthy as individuals.
Which is why in the religions they make, people tend to feel less worthy, just like themselves.

This is why the priest or priestess needs a king.
A king can carry out the commands of their desires, covered as spiritual revelation or prophecy,
while also being the face outwards of recognition among the masses,
so when things go bad the head of the king is put on a stake,
while the temples can rebuild after potentially losing their treasures.

The perfect king is somebody who is just smart enough to give orders and win battles,
but also dumb enough to not realize who actually has the real power.
Invoking fear of the deities in kings is an important task.

For example, Alexander the Great was tricked into thinking he was a son of Zeus-Ammon.
As a result of Alexander's battles, he became legend,
which was the priests and priestesses way of ensuring their grip on power.
One great king is always leveraged into manipulating future kings.

There is no thing about power in the ancient world that is particularly hard to handle e.g. for the women by the well.
After all, they have to deal with much greater complexity to make society function.
These women, with great cognitive and physical capacity, are more than capable to deal with power.

So, the trick that priests and priestesses use is to anoint a king,
followed by making the king deny the women by the wells access to education.
The lack of education keeps the women by the well incapable of seizing and holding onto power in society.
Education is closely linked with religion, to gate keep the knowledge from falling into the wrong hands.

This is where the expression "Knowledge is power" comes from.
In the ancient world, knowledge is regarded a way to climb up the social ladder and away from duties of carrying water.
Knowledge and education is deliberately kept away from those who serve the most important roles in society,
to be able to continue abusive behavior on a level that does not completely collapse the entire system.

### 5. Powerful men giving orders and guidelines for use of water

Let us say that there is a person A that is abusive toward women,
to a such extent that the women by the well refuses to give A some water.

The person A does not have any choice (except die), than to trick some person B that the women by the well obeys.
So, the women by the well is told the water is for B, but actually it goes to A.

After a while, when B spends twice the amount of water than usual,
the women by the well sends a spy to figure out where the water goes.
It has to be somebody who can run away when there is trouble, so it has to be a young person,
since the grown ups are in so good physical shape and with great stamina, that anyone grown up would only be beaten by somebody younger.
They can not send a young man directly, so they either send a young woman, or seduce a young man to spy for them.
The spy comes back and reports that person B gave water to person A.

All hell breaks loose.

If person B continues to give water to person A,
then the authority of the women by the well is undermined,
yet they can not afford to piss off B too much,
since B might bump into anyone that brings water,
or in the worst case: Sabotage the well.

The obvious solution to this problem is to put one man C in charge that people like person B obeys,
so that when A wants to pressure B for water, person B can excuse the situation to A on behalf of C.
Now, person B needs to go talk to C.

In this situation, one does not want to use the king,
because the king is on the side of the priests and priestesses,
that are plotting their next step to enrich themselves.
Involving the king in the situation almost always means more than less trouble.
The point is to cause less trouble, as the women by the well are in an inescapable situation, not more.
One way to cause more trouble is to walk straight into the trap set by some priest,
watching out for this exact opportunity.

This is why the women by the well need a powerful man that is not the king.
What they do, is making this man, the person C, a judge.

When B goes to C, there is a trial and they put forth evidence and witnesses.
The interest of C is getting to the bottom of the truth,
because if this role fails the whole society is at stake.
In the process, B has to submit to C's authority and judgement,
because the alternative is to die of thirst.

When C lacks information, he has to go to the women by the well, which might disturb their work.
So, the women appoints an advisor for C.
Officially, C is the nonofficial authority besides the king,
but unofficially, the official authority is the advisor for C.
To keep this under wraps, the easiest way is to match C against the woman they choose to marry him.
This way, C can function as a judge during the day and ask for advice by need in the evening, night or morning.

As a result, there is a "wise woman" that is officially outside the regular society,
but unofficially the most central authority figure in the whole society and most vital to its functioning.
She is usually married to the judge, which serves as an official front that gives guidelines for use of water.
The judge is powerful, to prevent anything from disturbing society in a significant way,
but also governed by a soft power behind, to keep in touch with the actual experience of handling water usage.

When the king cracks down on small villages, they go for the wise woman as an act of the highest brutality.
The wise woman is accused of some made-up crime and the king holds a fake trial,
mimicking the ones held by the judge, which is used as a part of the propaganda to scare people from rebelling.

On some rare occasions, the wise woman acts as the judge, for example Deborah in the bible.

### 6. Influence on spiritual and sexual language

Since all other people, than those working or watching out for kids, are not let nearby the well,
it means that going to the well is a form of social intrusion.
A common ritual for men when getting engaged, is to go to the well, looking for a woman to marry.

Using intrusive language to signify sexual or marital relationship is part of the culture in the ancient world.
When Jesus goes to the well in Gospel of John, he is not there to primarily still his thirst.
You can tell because he is not allowed to take water himself from the well.
Jesus needs to ask a woman to give him water, or else he would violate strong social norms.
The role Jesus plays here is someone that intrudes on the normal social order,
while at the same time respecting the bare minimum that was tolerable.
The point is that the author can make Jesus claim his message is the water of life,
the water that stills your thirst, forever.

What the author of John does here is reversing the role.
The well and the people bringing water from it are the very building block of what it means to be useful in society.
This need for belonging to society, is exploited, to claim a higher role of the priest or priestess.
Yet, everybody knows that priests or priestesses are useless, so the author points to Jesus himself as the source,
while in practice people need to go to the priest or priestess to get access to water of life.
It is a sign of people sharing this common understanding that the priest or priestess can not brag about their status,
but needs to present themselves as humble servants to the people that only does the will of their deity.
Otherwise, this would not be socially acceptable.

When Jesus goes to the well, this might be compared to something that ordinary men would not dare to do,
which is only done on special occasions, but it is precisely to make this seem like a special occasion the author chooses this act.
Yet, at the same time, the meaning carries layers of sexual intent,
that is used to bridge the language of spirituality and sexuality,
to make it more intoxicating and appealing to the audience.
It is also worth noticing that the woman by the well has no name and even mocking her for having previous sexual relationships,
while at the same time denying the mocking by presenting Jesus as tolerant toward her.
It is kind of like saying "You are worth nothing, but I am still able to tolerate you and give you salvation".
This gives Jesus the entire spotlight and glorifies this kind of abusive behavior as if it is something good.

The well is not associated with a good place, because it symbolizes the state of the entire surrounding world as barren and harsh for life.
However, within that landscape, the well is as good as it is going to get, because any deviation from the optimal action is a waste of energy.
So, when the author of John uses a well, it is because the author want to present the world as hostile to spiritual life.
The point of the author is:
Only Jesus can be the savior and people need to get this message to a such extent that they regard it as more important than actual water.

The particular way the author of John writes, when interpreting the well as tragedy,
is in the sense that there is no way out of it, using abusive language:
Jesus is the spiritual well and you should not spend nights worrying about an escape.
You have no choice but to provide the minimal amount of effort to stay alive and Jesus is willing to intrude and disturb to make it happen.
Jesus here is threatening to make you insane, hinting at the release of all your struggles, yet not delivering on any specific promise.

The woman by the well asks Jesus for the obvious:
Give me this water so I do not have to go here anymore.

I would bet that all the women by the well would agree,
but since the author of John is writing for an audience that recently gained access to higher education
and is seeking a way, away from the duty of carrying water,
this is a story where Jesus ignored the woman and her needs,
to elevate those who used this text, instead of elevating the woman.

No person who might have lived in history is more known than Jesus,
but we still do not know the name of the woman he met by the well.

I hope you think about this and do not just see a mirror of the modern mind living in a world with access to tap water and water bottles.
