# Semitic Mythical Origin of Jesus about Transfigured Reincarnation

To reconstruct 2nd century cultural literature context of Roman satire in Early Christian texts,
it is useful to construct a hypothesis that the story about Jesus has a Semitic mythical origin,
developed around themes of transfigured reincarnation.

Transfigured reincarnation is a religious belief system, where reincarnation does not mainly happen
due to rebirth cycles of human life and death, but between inanimate objects of important religious symbolism
and mediation of divine messages.

### Hebrew as a tool to promote Greek to Aramaic speaking people in Judea

In the 1st century, people who live in Judea transition from using Aramaic to using Greek.
This is one of the great puzzles in biblical scholarship,
because the history that is presented through Hebrew literature, is non-historical.

One scientific explanation is that the Pentateuch might have been a project at the Library of Alexandria.
This project sought to revive an ancient language, Hebrew, which reconstruction was largely based on Aramaic.

Politically, this text might have been used as a tool of cultural influence in the period when
people in Judea transition from Aramaic to Greek.
The power dynamics is that people perceive the reconstructed Hebrew as an older language than Aramaic,
but the text is so cryptic, that they use the Greek translation to convey the "true" meaning of it.

This way, Greek sneaks in as a Trojan horse, using Hebrew as the vehicle to enter the city.

### Who was Jesus originally? Was Jesus crucified?

One particular mystery is that Jesus never mentions Yahweh in Early Christian texts.
When Jesus answers the question where he comes from, he uses the term "Ouranos",
which can mean "heaven" or an ancient Greek deity.

Furthermore, Early Christians seems to believe that Jesus and Yahweh is the same divinity.
When did people start believing this, if Jesus was a historical person?

The name "Jesus" means "Yahweh saves" in Hebrew.
"Jesus" and "Yoshua" is the same name, as in the Yoshua that followed Moses.
There might also have been a Greek origin, as in "Jason" from the myth of the Golden Fleece.

The Jewish community in Alexandria began speaking Greek as early as 2nd or 1st century BCE.
Most people in Judea spoke Aramaic during this period.
A Greek origin of the name "Jesus" would suggest that the story of Jesus originated closer to Alexandria.

Since Jesus is identified with Yahweh among Early Christians,
it is worth to keep in mind that Yahweh was popular in Judea.
However, the story of Moses might have been a Hellenistic influence.

Temple washing rituals in Judea were centered on belief systems around a Yahweh.
At the time, people thought of Yahweh as an invisible giant with super-masculine attributes.

There is another piece of the puzzle, which is that Yahweh had a consort Asherah,
which in Hebrew texts was demoted.
Later, Early Christians use the Greek name "Zoe" to refer to Jesus' consort.

Jesus' crucifixion seems to be appearing after Roman persecution of Jews,
in a time period where many Jews were crucified for rebelling against the Roman empire.
If the story of Jesus has a Semitic origin, then it is less likely that Jesus was originally crucified in these stories.

This means that a Semitic origin of the story of Jesus
might have been without a crucifixion, a closer identity to Yahweh and where Asherah plays an important role.

### Asherah as a reincarnated goddess, through religious symbolism of the Menorah

Asherah is closely related to holy trees located around the Levant, where women gather to exhange knowledge.

The Tree of Life, which in Greek can be translated as "Zoe's Tree",
might have originally been both the Tree of Knowledge and the Tree of Life in the same religious symbol.

Over time, the Tree of Life becomes distinct from the Tree of Knowledge,
as a polemic against Asherah.
In some Hebrew texts, it is explicitly commanded that Asherah poles should be cut down.
It is currently uncertain what the specific religious symbolism of the Asherah poles had,
and whether it represented a goddess or some inanimate holy object.
Asherah poles become more stylized and might have been a precursor for the temple Menorah.

Early Christians believe that Zoe is a reincarnated goddess.
If Asherah and Zoe are related to each other, then in what sense could Asherah have been reincarnated?

One hypothesis is that both Zoe and Asherah are based on, or influenced, by the King Midas myth.
However, the problem currently is lack of scientific evidence to support this.

There is very little surviving historical data about the King Midas myth,
but according to some versions of it, Zoe might have been the daughter of King Midas that is turned into a golden statue.
Zoe gets baptized in a river in Turkey at the advice of Dionysus.

The King Midas myth was popular and perceived to be as old as Homeric tales, perhaps even older,
but it is uncertain how much of it is Hellenistic, or what degree there is a Semitic influence on the myth.

One hypothesis is that Zoe and Asherah is the same goddess,
that gets reincarnated through transfiguration instead of death: As a tree, or a statue, or a pole.
Thus, an Asherah pole might have been believed to be an inanimate state of the goddess Asherah.
During the period when people in Judea transition from Aramaic to Greek,
the name Zoe might have been used to refer to Asherah in living form,
while the name Asherah got a more passive or symbolic meaning associated with the trees, statues and poles.

The proximity of Asherah poles and later the temple Menorah, to the alter of Yahweh,
can be interpreted in the sense of "Jesus" as meaning "Yahweh saves".
So, the reincarnation through transfiguration, is Yahweh reviving Asherah to life from the inanimate state.
This way, Asherah comes to life, before she once again turns into a tree, a statue, or a pole.
The religious symbolism is about seasons of the year, where Asherah coming to life
represents the trees and plants that flowers during the spring.

The polemic against Asherah in Hebrew texts might have been a political motivation,
to integrate the religious rituals of people in Judea with Hellenistic ones.
To achieve this, Yahweh needs a son, because Yahweh is Zeus in Greek mythology.
The son of Zeus is Dionysus, which is the deity that gives King Midas the advice to baptize his daughter Zoe.
Dionysus literally means "son of Zeus" in Greek.
Thus, Asherah must be brought to life through a new deity, named "Jesus"
after the religious act of bringing her back to life, hence: "Yahweh saves" becomes the Jesus, son of Yahweh.

The temple Menorah becomes a more static symbol over time, but it also represents "The Spirit" of Yahweh.
That spirit is Asherah, which shares the same Hebrew root as when people later talk about "The Holy Spirit".
Yet, among Greek speaking Early Christians, which use a more Hellenistic religious language,
The Holy Spirit is clearly associated with Zoe.
In this form, Zoe is not a static religious symbol like the temple Menorah.
She is a living, breathing goddess.

The reason for this development might be that in the Hellenistic religious traditions,
Zoe is not reincarnated through transfiguration.
Zoe is more or less the same as other Greek deities.
This explains the polemic against Asherah:
Asherah is seen as an Eastern, Semitic influence.
Respected, but also feared.
Her power to reincarnate, the strong symbolism with springs and wine production,
the abilities to control lions, talk to snakes and spy through birds,
becomes a bit too much to handle for Hellenistic elites,
which world view has a history of demographic collapse.

Therefore, the Hellenistic elite has to make Asherah remain a static symbol,
while they offer a "better" replacement, Zoe, that is living, breathing, goddess,
not merely a powerful holy spirit that alternates metaphysically between life and death.

The Tree of Life, or "Zoe's tree" as it reads in Greek,
can be both thought of as a tree in the garden of Eden that is nurtured by Zoe,
the daughter of King Midas,
and Zoe herself being transfigured into an inanimate state as an Asherah, a holy tree.
This holy tree is the home of snakes, that people regard as their ancestors,
existing as temporary life forms before they get reborn as humans again.
Therefore, Tree of Life gets a dual meaning, both as a property, as part of a larger garden, and as a being in itself.

### Initiation of divine mediation

People believed that the whizzing sound of these tree snakes,
were the snakes talking to the Asherah, who rustled her leaves in response.
This was caused by the wind, who was seen as part of Yahweh's power as a storm deity.
Therefore, the increasing wind before storms was seen as a divine warning,
and the wind during lightning storms brought dangerous wild fires.

In the myth of Moses, Yahweh talks to Moses through a burning bush.
A burning bush might be thought of a wild fire,
and this is reflected in the symbolic candle lights of the temple Menorah.

Unlike the Hellenistic tradition, which uses a messenger, Hermes from Olympus, as mediator between humans and deities,
the mediation with the divine in Semitic culture is through the dynamic elements of nature: Wind, fire and water.
An interpretation of the name Asherah is "she who walks on water".
Later on, this might have been the origin of why Jesus walks on water as a miracle.

In the Book of Revelation, Jesus is an angel sent from heaven, kind of like Hermes.
However, Jesus also walks amidst the seven fires that represents seven churches.
This symbolism could have been a compromise between Hellenistic and Semitic traditions.
The particular language used in this text gives signals of how the deities at the time initiate mediation.

Yahweh is a storm deity, but his home is located at the top of mountains,
one reason why the temples where built on mountain tops.
People put up standing stones to represent the groundedness and connection to Earth.
Giant footprints point the direction to the temple, where Yahweh is believed to live as an invisible giant.
When Abraham gets angels as visitors, they are presented as travelers,
not as descending beings from heaven.
This might have been due to the Semitic tradition where deities are living amongst humans on Earth,
not looking down on them from heaven.
Over time, as Yahweh's size increases and through influence of Hebrew texts,
Yahweh's throne becomes heaven and Earth becomes his footstool.
In the temple, the Ark is the footstool of Yahweh.

Angels were generally thought of as sons of Yahweh,
so naturally, when a messenger comes from heaven,
in Hellenistic perspective that messenger must be a son of a deity.
Jesus as a messenger from Ouranos, is a son of deity in terms of his role as a messenger.
In a more Semitic tradition, Yahweh speaks through the elements of nature,
where inanimate objects become life, but returns to an inanimate state after divine mediation.

Therefore, it is possible that the stories about Jesus were of a Semitic origin,
where Jesus is seen as Yahweh's power that brings Asherah back to life,
which in the Hellenistic tradition was interpreted as Dionysus, who saves Zoe by advising King Midas to baptize her in a river,
but over time these stories developed into themes of temptation, persecution and trial.

### Temple washing rituals' influence on Early Christianity

In order to visit a temple, people used rituals of spiritual cleanliness.
There were designated areas around the temple which people could not enter,
unless they had performed the right rituals.

Moses takes off his sandals in front of the burning bush,
because the ground where he stands is holy.
Taking off your sandals is a sign of groundedness and holiness with Earth.

The water used for spritual cleansing, had to be from particular sources,
such as coming from a river in a particular way.
For example, it was not allowed to carry the water upstream.
The water might be lead into basins away from the river, as long it was downstream.

Jesus does not personally baptize anyone, but orders his disciples to do this task.
This is religious symbolism of abiding by the rule of use of downstream water.
The Water of Life (or Zoe), is Jesus himself.
Therefore, the disciples of Jesus in Early Christianity are the downstream authority, below Jesus.

It is similar to how Dionysus advises King Midas to baptize his daughter Zoe.
Dionysus does not baptize Zoe directly.
The priest or priestess is a person that functions as a mediator between the divine and humans.
The Holy Spirit is a fire given to the mediator, to bring humans back to Life (Zoe).

Without a mediator, there could be no possible violation of spiritual cleanliness.
The mediator's role is to create distance between the divine and people,
such that people are not allowed to question divine authority.
Surpassing the mediator is seen as trespassing into the most holy room of the temple, where Yahweh has his throne.

The distance that the mediator creates, is a clear language boundary,
where people learn which social limitations they have in the religious rituals they perform.
It functions as a divine test, or trial, of the person that is worshipping.

In the Book of Job, Satan is an adversary, an angel and a son of Yahweh.
Satan serves as the mediator between Yahweh and Job.
The task that Satan is given by Yahweh, is to test Job's faithfulness and righteousness.
Satan is given downstream authority from Yahweh.
Downstream authority might sometimes be thought of as evil, but still,
people were not allowed to question that authority.
This is why Satan can do evil, but still be regarded as a messenger of temptation from Yahweh.

When Yahweh's power to bring inanimate forms to life is associated with temptation,
it might be perceived as an evil force, depending on the situation.
Some Hebrew texts directly express in Yahweh's voice: "I create good and evil."

Disobeying authority, whether it was perceived as good or evil,
was thought of as a worse sin than merely doing evil.
This is also reflected in the story of King Saul, where King Saul is commanded to commit a genocide.
King Saul's punishment by the judge Samuel, is not for doing evil,
but for failing to obey the command by Yahweh.
This is part of the same religious language, where downstream water is associated with unquestioned authority,
that might be perceived as both good and evil.
If it is evil, then it is usually seen as a necessary evil.

Satan is seen as a downstream force of evil, that the apostle Paul, who seen himself as given higher authority, could command at will.

When Justin Martyr writes in the second half of the 2nd century,
he describes a Jesus that tortures unbelievers for an eternity.
Here, Jesus acts as a mediator, but the point is that the mediation is happening in Hell,
where people are not allowed to question or disobey that kind of lowest state of authority.
Lower states of authority were associated with more evil and less personal freedom.

This way of changing the authority that Jesus has, depending on context,
implies that Jesus is a mediator between Yahweh and humans.
Jesus is a force that acts out Yahweh's command, that might be both good and evil.
Over time, Jesus' role gets separated into a "purely good" role (Christ) and a "purely evil" role (Satan).
Originally, they were both sons of Yahweh, or angels, messengers from Ouranos (heaven).
Yet, in older Semitic tradition, Jesus is a force of Yahweh, who brings life or destruction through natural elements, such as wind, water and fire.
The meaning of "Yahweh saves" can mean a necessary evil act, not just a good act.
This is why ancient people pray, to appeal to the good sides of deities.

The resurrection of Jesus in Early Christianity is a Hellenistic translation fable,
that got attached extra meaning, because Yahweh's power to give life,
is Jesus applying that power to the absolute lowest state:
Executed by Roman soldiers, by request from a Jewish priestly class.
In this situation, the execution of Jesus is an unquestionable act.
It can not be thought of as something that could be questioned or disobeyed,
because it comes from the priestly class.
Like the water at the lowest state, it can not possibly be used for spiritual cleanliness.
However, by some miracle, Jesus is risen, so he can continue his role as a mediator between the divine and humans.

This kind of unquestionable downstream authority at, its lowest state, as a turning point for salvation,
is a cultural reflection of the Jewish people being displaced and persecuted by the Roman empire,
after losing wars of rebellion.

Early Christians allow people to convert without circumcision,
a practice that the Roman empire wanted Judea to use before the Bar Kokhba war.
This might be interpreted as Early Christians, with a Jewish background, giving in to Roman political demands.

In the symbolic language that is used in Acts of The Apostles,
Peter sees the conversion of gentiles as an impurity.
However, he is given a vision by Yahweh, that this lowered state of authority,
will be mediated through Peter's role in society.
Thus, Peter obeys, which is the most important part,
despite that he thinks of it as a necessary evil.
Peter's resentment against this act does not go away,
but he still does it because he thinks of it as a situation,
where the ritual water of baptizement is further downstream.

Despite that circumcision disappears during Early Christianity,
the ritual of baptizement still remains, because of the transformative function
it has in relation between circumcision and temple ritual washing.
The very precise authorative meaning of this downstream metaphor of good and necessary evil,
is what Early Christians believe is giving them permission to get rid of circumcision.

Early Christians do not take away the ritual that gives them divine permission to remove other rituals.
Another ritual that disappears is animal sacrifice, again using Jesus' downstream authority,
where Jesus becomes the lamb to be sacrificed himself.
Jesus at the lowest state of authority,
is used to remove other rituals that Early Christians no longer want to, or can, perform.

### The bride of Christ

The role Asherah plays in the proximity of Yahweh,
is the goddess that alternates through transfiguration between inanimate states and life.
In that role, Asherah communicates with snakes, the intermediate forms of life that ancestors go through,
before they once again get reborn as humans.
This becomes later the story of the garden of Eden, where Zoe's tree is split into two trees:
The Tree of Knowledge and the Tree of Life.
Eve talks to the snake in the garden and eats from the Tree of Knowledge.
This is a polemic against Asherah, where Eve follows the Hellenistic story of Pandora.
Just like Pandora was an earlier goddess, Eve is demoted, but still respected, as the Mother of Life.

This is another piece of scientific evidence that Hebrew is used as a tool to align Aramaic speaking people in Judea with Hellenistic culture.

Under Early Christianity, the power of transfiguration is perceived as mediated through Jesus' flesh and blood.
It symbolizes the lowest state of being, the ultimate act of evil, that is cannibalism.
Early Christians consume what represents Jesus' flesh and blood,
because they see it as the only religious act they can do, in that lowered state of being.

The communion with Christ, is a reinterpretation of earlier rituals of Asherah,
that also used cookies and wine.
Asherah is awakened back to life through the lowest state of being:
The inanimate form of life and the elements of nature.

Asherah is the wife of Yahweh.
However, since Jesus is a son of Yahweh, through his role as a messenger in Hellenistic tradition,
and not yet settled down as a prime deity,
his female companion becomes a promised bride.

Again, this reflects the lowered, but still unquestionable, authority of Jesus.
The bride of Christ is not a female warrior like Athena or Anat.
It is a representation of the church, that is living under Roman rule,
obeying the Roman emperor.

After multiple defeats against the Roman army,
the Levant goddesses worshiped by tribes that rebelled,
were seen as taken prisoners or captives.
It signals obedience toward a lowered state of authority,
which is not perceived as a good state to live under,
but as a necessary evil needed to survive.

When human tribes develop mythology, it reflects their collective self-esteem as a group of people.
The more ambitious and expansionist a tribe is, the more aggressive and violent their deities become.
The bride of Christ is not perceived as violent at the moment,
meaning that the short term future is not looking good for Early Christians,
but they see themselves as taking revenge in Revelation, after completed the wedding ritual.
The wedding ritual symbolizes the return of Asherah, when Yahweh gets back his wife and claim to a bloodline of divine rulers.

In the eyes of ancient people, the right to rule was perceived as access to women from certain bloodlines.
These women were important, to keep the confidence of the people.
The wife's duty was to produce a heir, to avoid a rebellion and future violent conflicts about power.
When a ruler could not produce a heir, this was interpreted as a bad omen.

It is more or less unthinkable for ancient people to not believe in divine offspring.
This is because their religions are a reflection of their own mental states.
However, without a goddess, there can be no offspring.
A bride goddess is kind of trapped into an eternal state of virginity.
This poses a serious challenge for Early Christians, because their main deity was Jesus,
who had yet to establish himself as a prime deity.
The solution was to spread through the mechanism of disciples, which traveled to new regions.
As more and more regions got Early Christians, people invented new stories about the disciples of Jesus.

The bride of Christ is a kind of a placeholder, for some future return to a more normal pantheon for ancient people.
Yet, the system of making up new disciples and stories about them,
worked so well that the priority was changed from establishing a pantheon, to continue expanding churches.
When Christianity became the official religion in the Roman empire,
it quickly started to persecute its competitors.
This could be because if Christianity truly established itself with a new pantheon,
then people among their competitors would claim that their own pantheon was older and hence the more "correct" version of religion.
This led to Christians not feeling fulfilled in their own belief system,
using this as an excuse to attack other religions around them.
The war against other religions kept continuing over centuries,
so the Christians never got to finish their project entirely.

### Getting rid of Zoe

After Christianity became the official religion of the Roman empire,
Zoe became a problem theologically, because she was a goddess in the waiting line for power.
Using Zoe as a prime deity would be an admission that there was nothing special about Christianity compared to contemporary religions.

Therefore, the Christians came up with the solution of simply getting rid of Zoe.
The Holy Spirit, due to its language of transfiguration between inanimate forms and life,
helped to make this choice, since it could be interpreted in the way that Zoe was not really a goddess, but merely a divine force.

This resulted in another problem, which was the Hellenistic background upon which Zoe was developed.
The association between Zoe and Hellenistic culture, made it difficult as people would reconstruct Zoe from similar patterns in Greek religion.

Another level of denial was added, to make the Hellenistic influence go away entirely.
The earlier polemic against Asherah, which originally was precisely designed to introduce Hellenistic culture in Judea,
was reinterpreted as a polemic against Hellenism.

The theology of Late Christianity retreats to a more Semitic perspective,
but at the same time, history is rewritten to remove the traces of Yahweh's wife: Asherah.

Traces of the Zoe from the King Midas myth, her baptizement in a river and Dionysus' advice,
were more likely than not, removed from historical records,
leaving us with the small bread crumbs that we have today.
What was once a whole religion, has been reduced to a few footnotes of history.

### Summary

The stories about Jesus, when considering the available scientific evidence we have about Early Christianity,
might have developed, as a relatively simple and straight forward hypothesis,
from earlier Semitic stories about Yahweh bringing Asherah back to life through transfigured reincarnation.

This explains, when put into historical context,
the baptism rituals performed by Early Christians,
consumption of Jesus' flesh and blood based on similar rituals for Asherah,
and finally: The bride of Christ as a lowered state of authority which is promised some kind o freturn to power,
but hard to actualize due to competition from other established pantheons.

It is a hypothesis that explain many of the more esoteric sides to Early Christianity,
in a way that fits archeological evidence about temple washing rituals.
The 1st century was a period when people in Judea transitioned from Aramaic to Greek.
In this period, the power elite was trying to integrate this culture into Hellenism.
Later on, when Christianity became the official religion in the Roman empire,
it erased the traces of this Hellenistic program from its historical records
and tried to rewrite itself historically as a Semitic religion.

This was a large success, in part because the power elite could continue using Hellenistic culture
as a way of distancing themselves from the masses, perceiving themselves to have access to the "true" world view.
Hellenistic culture was and is even today, centuries later, thought of as part of higher education in society.
Greek goddesses such as Athena and Sophia, are still popular, but less in a religious setting and used in a more cultural way.

Ironically, the attempt to remove Zoe, just emphasizes the earlier cultural relation to the process of transfigured reincarnation.
It seems to be a kind of self-ironic cultural process.
