# Garden of Eden as Nourishment Advice

To reconstruct 2nd century cultural literature context of Roman satire in Early Christian texts,
it is useful to form a hypothesis about the story of Garden of Eden as folktale nourishment advice.

The idea is that before Genesis was written, there was a folktale about nourishment advice,
where people are warned against eating too much wine grapes and instead recommended to eat more nuts.
This folktale influenced the way people interpreted the story of Garden of Eden,
since the meaning of Tree of Knowledge (wine grape) or Tree of Life (nuts) is not explained.
By default, when something is not explained in mythology,
we should believe that this was so common knowledge that it did not need explanation.
With other words, folktales about nourishment advice is the kind of common knowledge,
that could give context to the deeper spiritual meaning of the two trees in the Garden of Eden.

### 1. Platonic bias vs Seshatic bias

Platonic language bias tends to make people vulnerable to a peculiar class of errors when interpreting ancient texts.
People who are Platonic biased, put more emphasis on cognitive or mental sources to ground spiritual language.

We know from the mythology in Ancient Egypt that a deity's symbol could have materialistic meaning,
where the connection between the symbol and the authority of the deity is by causal relationships.
These causal relationships were part of the cultural understanding people had about themselves,
that trained their minds to see particular materials or items as means for social construction and personal health.
With other words, symbolism in the ancient world can have surprising connections for the modern mind,
which is often detached from the educational content of mythology and tend to see the world through a perspective of scientific value and information.
While in the ancient world, people actually had to develop mythology to make their complex social relationships as stable as possible, in order to survive.

For example, in the case of Seshat, an Ancient Egyptian goddess of knowledge, language and wisdom,
her symbol is the hemp plant:

A modern mind might view Seshat's symbol as some substance used to alter the consciousness, for example by admistering a drug.
This is because to us, knowledge, language and wisdom are grounded in the cognitive aspects of the human mind.
We see the human mind as an approximate version of a more idealized theoretical perfect rational mind with instrumental goals.
Yet, this lens is a result of hundreds of years of Intellectual History since the beginning of the scientific revolution.

The explanation of Seshat's symbol is much simpler than most people would have guessed:

1. From hemp, one can make rope
2. Using rope, one can measure things
3. From measurement, one gets numbers
4. From numbers, one gets knowledge

This way of thinking about knowledge, by crediting knowledge by causality,
is the mathematical dual language bias to the Platonic language bias of crediting knowledge by abstraction.
In Path Semantics, a field studying mathematical language design,
this duality between the two language biases is so important that Seshatic bias got its own name: Seshatism.
Seshatism vs Platonism is the most important dualities in Joker Calculus that is used to construct higher dualities from the two bases.

### 2. Wine production in Ancient Syria

Ancient Syria was a center of wine production, both in the Hellenistic period and the Roman empire.
While modern religions focus in great detail upon Israel and Judea as the source of divine knowledge,
in the ancient world Israel, and in particular Judea, was more like an economic backyard to Syria.

The region of Israel and Judea was mostly considered a bridge between Egypt and Syria.
Consider for example the stories about King David of Judea vs King Omri of Samaria.
One of the reasons that our cultural knowledge about Samaria is much worse than Judea today,
is because Samaria got big enough during King Omri's rule to be noticed and hence was later crushed in multiple wars.
Huge armies could march through Judea without even realizing that they were marching through a nation.
This was a source of embarrassment for the Judeans,
to the extent that they had a story about how one of their first great kings,
David, defeated the giant Goliath using nothing but a shepherd's weapon.
With other words, Judeans did not have the resources to equip their armies properly and had to tune their propaganda accordingly.
They were even defeated in a battle that Yahweh had promised they would win,
but Yahweh failed delivering on this promise because the enemy had iron chariots.

Wine production was the great economic engine that drove up wealth in Ancient Syria,
while smaller nations like Judea were jealous about this and big parts of their mythology
showed over time increased hostility toward Asherah, Yahweh's wife, that was a goddess of wine.

In the story of Garden of Eden in Genesis,
Eve is tricked by a snake and eats from the Tree of Knowledge,
before she gives to Adam.
The story does not say what kind of fruit the Tree of Knowledge carries.
However, based on the economic historic data we have about this region,
it would not be surprising that this fruit would be a wine grape.
Eve's act in the Garden of Eden reflects the economic jealousy of the elites in Judea,
toward Syria who had higher living standards, more developed mythology and a flourishing trading network.

Eve symbolizes Samaria and Syria, the woman or goddess of wine,
that Judea wants to conquer and make subservient to the man Adam,
who symbolizes Judea and is created from dirt and dust by Yahweh's own hand.
It is an expression of pride in the Judean nation, where people keep surviving thanks to religious zealotry.
These are the survivors that, being mostly ignored by other nations by not having a well supported army,
will shape the future landscape of Judaistic religions.

### 3. Nuts as dense nutrition and vital calories in a world with food scarcity

Nuts contain more nutritional value than most fruits.
Humans descend from great apes that lived in the jungle, eating nuts as part of their stable diet.
The human body is well adapted to consuming nuts and to open nut shells using tools and their opposing thumbs.

Historically, each time humans invented a new way to produce more food,
the number of people increased and surpassed traditional means of food production.
For example, using corn as crops made corn a greater part of the stable diet,
while the share of nuts in the stable diet decreased,
simply because corn was a new way to produce more food and hence sustain more people.
A greater population with an economy relying on great numbers of people,
meant that one could not go back to a stable diet with a greater share of nuts.

However, since the human body is better adapted to consuming nuts than corn,
this means that the rich, who maintain their wealth by supplying big armies with cheap corn,
can afford a diet with more nuts to stay healthy and strong to give orders,
or even fight a duel when honour demands it.

Nuts made the rich less vulnerable against diseases of malnutrition.
So, access to nuts was an upside to being rich.
However, the downside was also more access to wine, alcohol and intoxicating substances.
While nuts might have been considered almost exclusively good for your health,
wine grapes could be both good for your health, but with potential dangers associated with it.

### 4. Folktales about nuts being divine food providing eternal energy

Due to nuts being more nutritious than most other food,
soldiers quickly learned that nuts would provide them with more energy per unit weight,
while also taking up less volume under transport.

This could have resulted in folktales about divine food providing eternal energy, in the lens of nutritional content.
Such folktales might seem a bit strange today, since we live in a world with abundant access to food.
We tend to think about divine food as some kind of bringing magical transfiguration of the body or mind to access and dominate the spiritual realm.
However, in the ancient world, divine food might take on a more pragmatic and practical relationship to the world.
Everybody understood at the time that more nutritious food kept people alive.
They saw plenty of examples where people died as result of lack of access to nutritious food.
This was not something that people needed explicit instruction about, because it was just common knowledge.

Yet, for the rich that also had more access wine and potentially harmful substances,
a cautionary folktale could provide help to choose wisely in a moment of temptation.
This is why nuts as divine food is relevant,
not because people lacked this as common knowledge,
but because it is contrasted with the possible harmful side effects of eating too much of wine grapes.
The folktale functions as a reminder of wise course of action, pointing toward the normal and stable diet as the answer.

The greater temptation, the more resolute the language in the folktale is needed, to balance against potential harmful actions.
This means that nuts might not only been perceived as being more nutritious,
but also as a possible source of divine nature itself, from which all life springs.
A such use of spiritual language might help people overcome their addictions.

### 5. Zoe, Eve and Nut

In the beginnig of Gospel of John, the Greek original text uses "Zoe" which means "Life".
Some Early Christian sects teach Zoe as a divine consort of Logos, the Greek philosophical concept associated with Jesus.

In Simonianism, a sect of Early Christianity that had Simon Magus as savior figure instead of Jesus,
Helen of Tyre is the consort of Simon Magus.
She is reincarnated in history from the creation of the world and lives various lives, among them is Helen of Troy.
In some sense, Helen of Tyre is the mother of all humans and in a broader context of all life.

In Genesis, the mother of all life is Eve, which eats the fruit from the Tree of Knowledge.
If the Tree of Knowledge is a plant that carries wine grapes,
then it is possible that the Tree of Life is a plant that carries nuts.
Genesis might have been written around the time the Library of Alexandria is built,
by some person who is biased toward Platonism,
crediting knowledge by abstraction and seeing knowledge in the context of mind altering substances.

Another translation of "Tree of Life" from Ancient Greek is "Zoe's tree" using the Greek philosophical concept.
This could be interpreted as "Eve's tree" in the sense that she is the mother of all life.
However, when thinking about Eve as Asherah, Yahweh's wife and goddess of wine,
the phrase "Eve's tree" becomes ambiguous with "Tree of Knowledge".
This ambiguity might have been intentional, to play these two meanings against each other as some kind of spiritual mystery.

Yet, when using the Egyptian parallel of Asherah, Nut, the goddes of the night sky and ruler of the divine afterlife,
the phrase "Nut's tree" immediately carrier over to "Tree of Nut": A tree that carries nuts.
This interpretation might be just a historical coincidence, but it makes sense in terms of nuts being more nutritious than other food,
divine food in the sense of providing eternal energy and nourishment as meaning both food and motherly care.
The goddess of the night sky, Nut, was also portrayed as a cow, which was an important source of diary products.

In the same way that the specific fruit on the Tree of Knowledge is lost to history,
the specific fruit on the Tree of Life also lost.
One reason for this transition where these fruits become more abstract,
could be that Platonic bias influences religions to a greater degree,
plus that there are multiple products that might be perceived as fitting in this duality
and people vary their interpretation according to their own motivation of business profits and hoarding wealth.
Wine grapes vs nuts illustrates one such possible interpretation.
