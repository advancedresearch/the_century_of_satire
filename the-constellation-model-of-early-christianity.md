The most popular savior figure in Christianity is Jesus, who most people today believe lived in Galilee and Judea in the early 1st century.

However, this model is not that useful when studying history and texts written by Early Christians.
Most surprising is this fact: Jesus was NOT the central savior figure in Early Christianity when it starts spreading!

### 1. The dynamics of religious beliefs in the ancient world

Deities in the ancient world age and retire, sometimes they even die.
This is a very different dynamics than how people perceive religion today.
The reason that deities in the ancient world age, is because people relate to them as a reflection of themselves.
They had little use of some abstract, all-powerful and all-knowing Platonic deity existing outside space and time.
Deities for ordinary people were super-heroes and sometimes villains and anti-heroes.
They were connected to natural events, such as thunder, Earth quakes and water.
All things that they observed the world was made of, was in some sense the result of a deity.
This includes things like Love, Scripture and Wisdom.

Most people that have grown up in Christianity today have heard about The Tree of Life.
However, did you know that "Life" in this context was the name of a deity?
She is called "Zoe", which in Greek means "Life".

Hello, Zoe!

Zoe was one the central deities in Early Christianity.
She was even more important than Jesus, because Jesus was just one of several savior figures,
while Zoe was in all of them.
Since Zoe was Life and every Early Christian sect worshipped The Tree of Life.

Did you know that Moses was a savior figure?

The word "Pagan" was used by city people about those who lived outside cities in the country side.
This Wizard religion, or Magi, that people in the city developed, was in the ancient world the equivalence of modern day universities.
People learned medicine alongside religion and other topics such as math and history.

It is believed that Galen, the emperor's physician (and the highest Magi of them all), wrote around 600 books.

### 4. The Keftiu people, Medea, Ancient Greece and Christ

Mystery religions go way back to the Late Bronze Age, possibly even further, e.g. the Minoan civilization.

The Minoan civilization is a term that was invented in modern time. They called themself "Keftiu".

In the electromagnetic spectrum, colors vary from blue (400 nm) to red (800 nm).
There is no natural color on this spectrum with a single frequency that corresponds to purple.
However, the human brain perceives the color purple when blue and red is superimposed with each other.
With other words, the purple color is an imaginary color, unlike all the other colors on the spectrum.

Depending on the brightness of purple, we call it violet, magenta or pink.
They are all words for the color purple, but sometimes we use purple to refer to a specific color.

The characteristic trait of purple is that it is an artificial color that rarely occurs in nature.
So, when you create a color that ordinary people can not see in nature,

### 5. Natural change in religions leads to losing historical records of its own origin

Religions and culture change naturally over time.

People living the Middle Age discovered ancient ruins and thought that this must have been a lost civilization with magical powers.
What they did not know, was that these ruins were simply traces of earlier generations, by their own people,
that have a seemingly different culture and religion, due to the passage of time and cumulative changes.

What happens in Early Christianity is that as time goes by,
one group of people starts to think their own mystery religion was the true history of Israel,
while the another group from the same movement starts to think they have been converted to a new religion from other nations.
The first became what we know as modern Jewish religion,
while the second became an umbrella of religious traditions spreading to the entire world: Christianity.
However, the actual history is that both groups belonged to the same people that used the same texts for religious purposes.

It is precisely because one view of history becomes dominant that deviates from actual history,
that another group of people can believe they are outside of actual history.
The latter people tend to fail to recognize their own culture in historical evidence.
Furthermore, as time goes by and the way of living is adapted to migrating somewhere else and marrying into new ethnic groups,
the roots to history becomes eroded and people feel detached from history,
yet at the same time keeping religious traditions that goes back to the origin.

It is in this sense that Christians fails to recognize that e.g. the Early Christian tradition goes
back to the Qumran sect, that used the Septuagint and Hebrew manuscripts that later became part of the Masoretic tradition.
The answer to whether Greek or Hebrew came first is not either/or, but both/and.
Greek plays a more important role than Hebrew in Early Christianity, because Hebrew was a dead language at the time.

There are nuggets of historical value in the Greek and Hebrew texts, but overall it is a myth.
There was no historical Moses and possibly not even a historical David.
The texts are produced in the tradition of mystery religion and some of the stories are not meant to be interpreted as historical events,
but as spiritual tools in this religious tradition.

People in the ancient world do not have large libraries and the modern scientific lens that people use today.
To them, the distinction between a hypothesis and a theory was blurred out,
so when they edited or wrote new texts, they were thinking that these corrections would provide better historical accuracy.
However, when you have bad information in, you tend to get bad information out.

As one guy, who controlled audio on concerts, said: "Shit in, shit out."

This lesson is something people might take with them when trying to reconstruct history:
Sometimes, there is nothing to repair, because it is beyond repair.

### 6. Do not focus on savior figures - look at the use of holy texts instead - when putting the pieces together

The most painful lesson learned from working on this project,
that seeks to reconstruct 2nd century cultural literature context of Roman satire in Early Christian texts,
is that focusing too much on savior figures is the greatest beginner mistake that anyone can do.

The problem with savior figures is that everybody have their own version of how it is supposed to be like,
because we have different backgrounds and experiences in life that influences how we model other people,
including savior figures in myths.

People are used to think about religious traditions in the sense of savior figures and used this definition to trace historical events.
However, a much better definition is to use a constellation of holy texts and see who uses these texts over time in a religious setting.

This means, that ordinary people can swap out savior figures in the ancient world while keep using the same texts over time.
It gives a more robust model where e.g. Yahweh can be good sometimes and evil other times, depending on people's personal experiences.
Jesus can be good, Lucifer can be bad, but also Jesus can be bad and Lucifer can be good, both fighting over the right to the title "the morning star" (the planet Venus).

This way of thinking allows the possibility that some Early Christians view Lucifer as their savior figure.

The constellation model works better because it is like the night sky of stars, where each star represents some holy text.
In addition to the stars, there are planets, the moon and the sun, representing more important texts shared by many,
and most importantly the planet Venus, that makes one mystery religion a little different from everyone else's.

In addition to the Septuagint and some Hebrew manuscripts, the Sibylline oracles play a major role in Early Christianity.
People go to these oracles and record down what they are saying,
spending countless nights pondering upon the meaning behind these mystical and esoteric expressions of thought.
Perhaps this is a method to get under the skin of the human psychological makeup and reach the unconscious Self.

When tracing the use of holy texts as constellations that people navigate after,
a different picture emerges of Early Christianity where Jesus is not the major savior figure when Early Christianity spreads.
If Jesus was a historical figure with worshippers, then there was probably just a tiny sect during the 1st century.

There was probably not a large sect worshipping Jesus that died out in war against the Roman empire,
because other sects in Early Christianity, e.g. Simonianism, spreads successfully.
However, it might also be the case that Simonianism had less problems with the Roman empire by portraying itself as originating from Samaria.
In the 2nd century, after the Bar Kokhba revolt, we see a shift where people convert from Simon Magus to Jesus, e.g. Marcion of Sinope.

It is possible that there was no historical Jesus worshipped as a savior figure in the first place,
but people have used sources from Josephus to start new mystery cults.
Both Simon Magus and Jesus might have been taken from Josephus.
Josephus evidently know about texts that overlap with the Septuagint and Hebrew manuscripts,
so he can be placed within the same religious tradition as Early Christians and Jews.

Simply attributing one's own history to Abraham or Moses, puts a person within the same religious tradition.
This is important for people to understand, because in contrast, there are other religious traditions outside Abraham.
Just like Moses, Abraham probably never existed historically, but there can still be nuggets of historical value in the texts.

There are also people who worship Enoch as a savior figure and puts him above Moses.
New texts about Enoch are written at the same time as Early Christianity is developed.
This means, that one can not define Early Christianity as a tradition that exclusively builds on the Moses tradition.

Another savior figure is Melchizedek, which the Book of Hebrew in the New Testament is syncretised with Jesus.
Melchizedek comes from a tradition that traces its origin back to an alternative to Abraham.
Abraham gets blessed by Melchizedek, but this was likely added by worshippers of Melchizedek.

When you look outside the lens of defining people in this religious tradition through following a single savior figure,
