# Zoe - The Muse of Aristotle

To reconstruct 2nd century cultural literature context of Roman satire in Early Christian texts,
it is useful to form a hypothesis of Zoe as being a Muse of Aristotle based on the myth of King Midas.

In Ancient Greek, there were nine Muses standardized in the classical period:
Calliope, Clio, Polyhymnia, Euterpe, Terpsichore, Erato, Melpomene, Thalia, and Urania.

The first Muse, Calliope, is the goddess of epic poetry and was portrayed holding a copy of the Odyssey,
an epic poem by Homer about the war on Troy.

The war on Troy plays a key role in Simonianism, because the highest goddess Helen of Tyre
was though to have been reincarnated since the creation of the universe and lived among other lives as Helen of Troy.

The parallel character of Helen of Tyre, that is by some Early Christians believed to be the consort of Logos, is Zoe.
However, where does this Zoe come from?
While there is a connection to the war on Troy and Muses in Simonianism, Zoe is not part of that line of thought.
Helen of Tyre was not always thought to be a historical person,
but something like a Muse or Holy Spirit that came out of the body of Simon Magus when performing miracles.
In that context, Zoe is obviously associated to the Holy Spirit and could be a kind of Muse goddess, but from where and by whom?

The idea is that when bringing together Zoe and Logos, one gets Zoology.
Aristotle was the world's first Zoologist and is a candidate for this connection.
He could be basing this sort of intellectual Muse on the myth of King Midas, that turns his daughter Zoe into gold.

### 1. The myth of King Midas - a moral story about how increasing wealth is not the same as a good life

There are three different kings that are associated with the King Midas myth.
All of them are believed to ruled over Phrygia.

1. The first king might have lived in the 2nd millenium BC
2. The second king might have lived in the 8th century BC
3. The third king might have lived in the 6th century BC

In some stories, King Midas is getting a wish granted by Dionysus.
Midas wished that everything he touched would turn into gold.
The king dances of joy in the garden of his daughter Zoe (Life) and touches the plants.
However, when dinner time comes, Midas discovers that he has no food to eat,
because he gets it from Zoe's garden.
Sad and hungry, Midas becomes depressed and sits down on his throne.
However, Zoe discovers that her plants have turned into gold and runs to her father Midas crying.
She embraces him, but at the touch, Zoe turns into a golden statue.
Midas is heartbroken and asks Dionysus for help.
Dionysus tells him to wash the gold off in the river Pactolus.
Midas baptizes Zoe in Pactolus and she comes back to life.

It is not difficult to interpret this story as an early myth of washing ritual to cleanse oneself of sin.
A sin might have been thought of as something hurting inside the heart,
which was believed to be the center of reason by Aristotle.
The heart has turned into gold (that's why people say a heart of gold about a good person, which might have originally been a joke),
so the person needs to perform a washing ritual in the mystery cult they belong to.

This is clearly the language of mystery religion where having riches is not regarded the same as having a good life.
The economics is pretty simple, since the priests take money from people and in return offer them the eucharist ritual.
A further simple connection is that the drink is believed to cure the heart, by claiming the fluid originates from the holy river in some myth.
When it comes down to it, this is the mystery religion claiming that they are the only ones that can help people living a good life.

### 2. The mystery of a good life in Aristotle's works - can humans be more than animals?

Aristotle is not exactly famous for being the kind of person that falls for myths made by mystery religions.
Actually, he is regard as one the most dull authors of the entire ancient world.

However, if there is anything that Aristotle is obsessed by, it is to figure out how animals and life works in general.
If you interpret the myth of King Midas from Aristotle's perspective,
then King Midas just represents a solution to some problem when life goes wrong.
Aristotle was more concerned about life in the sense of daily activity and cycles of birth and death.
So, he might have been fascinated by the Zoe character, that represents the real philosophical puzzle to him: Life itself.

If you ask the question "What is the meaning of life?", in Ancient Greek, this is the same as "What is the meaning of Zoe?".
The name Zoe is not separated from the word life, but people used it with different meaning depending on context.
This leads to a recursive problem, because "What is the meaning of Zoe?" in this ambiguous sense,
also means to separate the meaning of Zoe as a goddess or Muse, from the non-spiritual term of life.

When going in this direction, one can see how people might perceive non-spiritual life as lacking meaning in some spiritual way.
What they need they invent, so Zoe becomes the life-force that is added to life-without-meaning to bring it back to life.
Zoe, when she is baptized and brought back to life, without any King Midas, is the spirit of coming back to life, the cycle of death and rebirth (reversing the order of ordinary life).
Hence, she is in some sense given by Dionysus that by his blood being wine produced from nature,
gives up his blood to humans, so his blood transfigures into Zoe in the participants of the eucharist ritual.

One might think that people in the ancient world saw people as better than animals through superior intellect.
However, they might as well regarded wine production as something that makes a similar difference.
After all, no animal the people living in the ancient world knew about, could make wine.
The sacred knowledge of wine production was thought to come from some deity, e.g. Dionysus.
Consider that Aristotle thought the center of reason was the heart,
people might have not formed very good ideas about what intellect means yet.
Zoe in this sense represents the mystery of a different kind of Muse,
one not just for art and poetry and specific sciences such as astronomy, but for pure intellect itself.
She becomes married to Logos and these two words together becomes Zoology, the study of life and animals in particular.

This is why Aristotle not only works on understanding animals, but also what makes people different from animals in nature.
It was understood at the time that what enlightened a person, was education,
but nature in the sense of animals would become for Aristotle a meaning of limits to potential.
This view of limited potential influenced how Aristotle regarded women, as having less potential in education than men in this sense.

So, Aristotle wants society to be controlled by men and not women.

While one might interpret this idea as misogyny on behalf of Aristotle,
he might actually just feel more pity towards women,
which would be more cruel because he does not even see himself as a women-hater.
That women would have less potential and therefore had to stay in their place, below men but above slaves.

The last part about slaves, kind of undermines the entire idea,
since male slaves being below women would contradict the idea that women have less potential than men.
However, this was kind of how screwed Aristotle's thinking was, more or less.
People in general were not much better, but there were people in the ancient world who were against slavery.
Unfortunately, Aristotle was not one of them, which would have severe consequences for later history.

### 3. Aristotle's teachings propagating to the Library of Alexandria - founded inspired by the Muses

To make a long story short: Aristotle becomes the teacher of Alexander the Great.
Alexander the Great dies and leaves a big portion of his empire to his general, Ptolemy I Soter.
Ptolemy I Soter and his son Ptolemy II Philadephus founded an institution, Mouseion,
based on inspiration by the Muses (as you might guess).
One of the things that Mouseion built was the Library of Alexandria.

So, the language of the Muses gets passed down through generations into the time
when this approach to Zoe, as being a vital life-force yet submissive to men, is going to really spiral out of control.

### 4. The Septuagint as a production in the Library of Alexandria - Tree of Life (Zoe's tree) in the Garden of Eden

Ancient Hebrew is a dead language.
There are probably very few people who read this language at the time of the Library of Alexandria.
However, it does not mean that nobody knew the language.
People at the Library of Alexandria could even read cuneiform,
an ancient writing system for clay tablets passed down from the Sumerian civilization,
which is multiple times further back in time for these people than we are to Library of Alexandria today.

Safe to say, these people know what they are doing,
and we can find the same Sumerian king list from clay tablets in the book of Genesis,
where descendants of Adam are pretended to be from this bloodline...
except that it is in the wrong millenium.
So, this makes it likely that Septuagint was written somewhere around the time of Library of Alexandria
and even perhaps in this specific intellectual culture,
where both knowledge about cuneiforms and Hebrew manuscripts existed at the same place in space and moment in time.

It is very unlikely that Hebrew manuscripts were composed as a cannon in this time,
because we find no traces of any famous biblical names, such as Noah, Abraham or Moses anywhere before this period.
At the same time, the Dead Sea Scrolls suggest that there were some Hebrew manuscripts around or being written.
This means, that the cannon which became the Septuagint could have been partly originating, composed and put together as a production.

From this step, it is just a small step further to see the Garden of Eden story as propaganda made by people knowing about Aristotle's philosophy.
Basically, women should not eat from the Tree of Knowledge (education) which fruit is a wine grape,
representing the blood from Dionysus that transfigures into Zoe among participants.
Zoe is separated from Tree of Knowledge as its own tree, a Tree of Life (Zoe's tree).
The woman Eve, like Pandora, brings evil to the world by being curious and she is prevented from accessing Zoe by a guarding angel.
With other words, they cut off Eve from her supply of Zoe.
She can not simply get baptized to wash away the gold in her heart any longer.
The curse is pain by child birth, which is like saying women should just care about giving birth and not get an education.

While this seems a stretch, remember that people at the time regard Isis and Medea as teachers of magic,
or Magi, which an umbrella term for people practicing many sub-disciplines of knowledge.
It was not like all religious traditions saw women getting an education as a negative thing.
The real trouble starts after Pandora and Eve, as putting the blame for evil symbolically on women.
Every shit-cult has ever since tried to prevent women from having an education.

However, there is a technical complexity to this picture.
In the Jewish religion, the fall of sin is not by Eve, but when angels get children with women.
This is not taking away the cause of pain by child birth as punishment for women being curious.
The Jewish religion evolved alongside Early Christianity, which both came from the same religious tradition.
One can interpret the Early Christian fall of sin as equally authentic to the historical origin.
It is just that different sects have different views when this fall of sin happens in the text.

### 5. Early Christians interpreting Zoe through the lens of the Septuagint and Aristotle's works

After the fall of the Byzantine empire, scholars escaped to Western Europe and brought new texts of Aristotle with them.
This suggests that the much earlier network of information in the ancient world regarding scholarship is not good.
The reason Western Europe cares about Aristotle, at all, is probably because they get handed down texts over generations.

One hypothesis is that Christianity gets access to Aristotle when Constantine the Great makes it the official religion of the Roman world.
However, the capital that Constantine establishes, is located exactly at the same spot as the center of the later Byzantine empire:
The capital of the Byzantine empire is Constantinople, the city established by Constantine the Great.

This leads to a conondrum that if people in Western Europe received texts of Aristotle from Constantinople,
they would have access to the information network to supply them with more texts than they had right after the fall of the Byzantine empire.
With other words, a more likely hypothesis is that works of Aristotle they know about is passed down by generations.

This puts Aristotle right in the center of the evolving Early Christianity.

While one might think about Zoe as Aristotle's Muse as being too speculative,
it is not impossible that Aristotle had great influence and that some Early Christians thought about Zoe in a similar way.
Perhaps the Tree of Life (Zoe's tree) was simply a historical coincidence that people connected it with Zoology later,
to give it the particular meaning of a reincarnated goddess that is the consort of Logos.

The fact that Helen of Tyre works a little differently in Simonianism,
might be a hint of Zoe as some kind of invention that Early Christians make to compete for followers.
In that sense, there might be no tradition being passed down in a continuous line of people from Aristotle's work,
yet at the same time, the close gap in language and use words can result in this idea being reinvented multiple times.

Later, when people start to regard Ancient Hebrew as the "authentic" language in Western Europe,
they dismiss Zoe as a divine concept and try to reverse engineer the Septuagint to see how it fits into a Hebrew tradition.
While in Eastern Christianity, Zoe might carry a more similar meaning to the original concept,
that helped keeping the view of the Septuagint as the more authentic text.

So, instead of thinking of Zoe as an idea that appears and vanishes once,
it might have kept coming back repeatedly and later dismissed at each turn,
functioning more as a Muse over time, kept a more silent a private worship for individuals.
An intellectual inspiration to find out the "secret history" of Christianity and the meaning of life (Zoe).
