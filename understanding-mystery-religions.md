# Understanding Mystery Religions

To reconstruct 2nd century cultural literature context of Roman satire in Early Christian texts, it is useful to understand mystery religions.

Mystery religions were the forms of highest entertainment in ancient world:

1. Coming of age
2. Education
3. Propaganda

Lot of people try to understand individual rituals or specific beliefs in mystery religions,
as if they hold the key to understand the mystery, while overlooking the greater picture.

Mystery religions were profitable businesses that functioned more or less the same way,
but selling slightly different products (the savior figures).

### 1. Coming of age

The main product that a mystery religion sells is initiation into adulthood.
Those who were not initiated were considered children by those who were initiated.
Only by being initiated, one could belong among the social exclusive group that identified themselves with some savior figure.

Humans have a natural psychological need to belong somewhere.
This need was exploited by mystery religions to create a form of entertainment
that combined horror, humour and epic genres that promised in the end that people would feel they belonged.
People that went through initiation did not know what to expect, but heard other people bragging about it,
but they knew that it would be safe, so the whole point was to overcome their own fears and anxiety.
By confronting their own fears and anxiety, they could get to the reward in the end: Adulthood.

Once people went through initiation, they had a conversation starter with people who went through the same rituals or similar ones.
This was an important part of gatekeeping social resources in the ancient world.
Therefore, adulthood was opening doors in the social and economic world.

### 2. Education

One can think about mystery religions as overlapping with education,
but the purpose was not education for some profession like today, which at the time used master-apprentice training.
Education in this sense was to learn the culture that connected various theatrical plays and religious rituals.
The deities worshipped in the ancient world were part of an inter-connected web of stories and songs.

Various plays and songs hinted at something beyond the visible world.
In order to understand these ideas, people needed some education.
People without education were seen as unsophisticated,
because they could not understand the hidden meaning buried in the message of various forms of entertainment.
By making initiated people look for hidden messages and spiritual meaning in the controlled mediums provided by the empire,
was how the elite controlled smart people mentally, while laws prevented the ignorant masses from rebelling.

### 3. Propaganda

The ancient world was a very brutal place, because the economy relied on slave labour and used wars to pay for the empire.
It was impossible to run a such complex social structure without propaganda.

Mystery religions did not try to figure out how the world worked scientifically.
They doubled down on the rethoric of the official propaganda of the empire,
but presenting themselves as counter-culture and used heavily sexual and violent language.

One can think about it as a form of escapeism that bring people deeper into the same ideology,
instead of training them to think critically about the world and their own society.
The ideological difference that mystery religions presented in relation to the mainstream ideology,
was to put themselves as adults, the people who knew how the world actually worked, above the children who were ignorant.

So, this sense of knowing how the world actually worked was understanding how slavery and exploitation of poor people was spiritually necessary.
In a modern sense, we would characterize this activity as corruption, cruelty and brainwashing.
