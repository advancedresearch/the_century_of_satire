# Why People are Often Surprised About History

To reconstruct 2nd century cultural literature context of Roman satire in Early Christian texts,
it is useful to consider reasons why people are often surprised by history.

Early Christianity is a religious movement that evolves an identity in the 2nd and 3rd century that we later associate with Late Christianity.
However, while people today think that this movement is about spreading the gospel of Jesus,
this mental model can cause people to be surprised by what history actually shows.

One of the reason that the mental people most people usually fails to meet the expectations of history,
is because the people in Early Christianity are very much reacting to what they perceive as history themselves.
With other words, these people do not think they are better or more knowledgable than other people in general (they all claim to be superior).
They do not have direct access to divine information sources and certainly never document any observation of some miracle.

Yet, the modern myth of Early Christianity propagates the impression that Early Christians are martyred in large numbers
and stick together despite their difficulties.
The reason people get this impression is partly grounded in history, but for the wrong reasons:

1. Many Early Christians were ethnically from the same region and culture as Jews (Early Christianity and the Jewish religion split at later point in time)
2. There is a lot of political tension between this region and the Roman empire during the 2nd century (e.g. Bar Kokhba revolt)
3. A devastating plage, the Plague of Galen in 165-180 CE that kills 5-10 million people, boosts the spread of Early Christianity due to stories of healing miracles

When taking these three things into account, it might seem to many people living in the Roman empire that the deities are punishing them,
or that they worship the wrong deities and this causes people to convert to Early Christianity.
At the same time, Early Christianity evolves from its polytheistic roots to becoming more and more monotheistic.
The authorities are observing this trend and worried about revolts, so they hit hard back on some Early Christians (e.g. Justin Martyr).
This reaction only fuels the movement and leads to the spread of Christian sects without control,
which the Roman empire weakened by the plague, wars and assassinations of emperors are unable to stop.

Yet, it is also important to realize that this is not unique to Early Christianity, but something that happens between multiple competing sects in mystery religions.
For example, the religious movement that worshipped Apollonius of Tyana.

However, when going back to the earlier phase of Early Christianity, toward the beginning and middle 2nd century,
there is not much we know about ordinary and poor Christians.
Actually, we do not have a single name of an ordinary and poor Christian from the entire 2nd century.
This is why The Century of Satire project is about trying to understand the texts Early Christians write from a perspective of contemporary literature.
These people do not have access to divine revelation, yet they claim to have it,
but for the reasons they write these texts, it seems very much because they are responding to criticism or commenting on their own culture and history.

The idea is that to develop a better model of actual history,
one needs to consider what it means to criticize contemporary authors and to comment on culture and history.
For example, the beliefs that people express are like philosophical positions with a broader reference to other philosophers and beliefs.
These texts are meant by authors to be understood by other people like themselves, who share a common background knowledge.
Because people are separated from this shared common background knowledge today, the mental model they try to fit onto history leads to surprises.

### 1. The eruption of Mount Vesuvius

In 79 CE, Mount Vesuvius erupted and killed entire cities.
This is one of the deadliest eruptions in history.

Theologically, this eruption became subject to one of the central topics for theses when criticizing and defending positions.
In the New Testament, the book The Acts of the Apostles dedicates large sections to defend Christianity using the eruption of Mount Vesuvius.

The way Early Christians defend their beliefs in Acts using the eruption of Mount Vesuvius,
is by having Paul warning Felix and Drusilla of divine consequences for their marriage.
Drusilla was possibly killed by the eruption, with her son Agrippa.
Agrippa is one of the few people known to have died in this eruption.

However, Drusilla is not the entire story of this debate about the eruption of Mount Vesuvius.
Pliny the Elder, a Roman author, naturalist, natural philosopher,
and naval and army commander of the early Roman Empire, and a friend of the emperor Vespasian,
also died during an attempt to rescue his friend Rectina and her family.

Pliny the Younger, the nephew of Pliny the Elder, survived the eruption and wrote about it.
This testimony is the only surviving written record of it.

Felix and Drusilla is written about by Josephus.
The eruption of Mount Vesuvius is written by Pliny the Younger.
Both authors are used by Early Christians in attempt to defend their beliefs against criticism.

To understand this debate, it is important to understand why people are using historical sources in religious discussion.
Citing historical sources gives better first impression in the contemporary philosophical literature.
One would think that people just have their faith and by coincidence they their history is recorded by somebody else.
However, at the time it is important for people to convince others to gain followers,
so by reversing the direction, finding historical sources to construct narratives around and center it on their own faith,
is a better model to explain why people participate in such debates in the first place.

Simply put:

    Historical sources => people making stuff up out of thin air

When people do not get the historical context of the debate, they do not actually understand the dynamics of these back-and-forth theses that people present.

The book of Acts is simply a frozen moment in this debate that was spread over multiple texts.

In the end, the goal, the reason people wrote the book of Acts,
is to convince people, that already know about the eruption of Mount Vesuvius,
that this particular mystery religion is the correct one.
Or, at the very least, build up more religious tolerance toward a specific group of people.

However, the book of Acts also attacks Simonianism, by presenting Simon Magus as a person attempting to buy the Holy Spirit.
This could be because the authors want authorities to attack Simonians instead of themselves.
It is unlikely they wrote it to convert Simonians to their own religion, because Simonians would just be offended.
Offending people is a bad strategy when attempting to convert them.

It is also important that the book of Acts is written in the context of the Hellenistic culture that people are part of at the time.
The words by Jesus to Paul is a direct quote from a story about Dionysus.
This might be an attempt to make people identify the savior figure with Dionysus, that was hugely popular and ancient.

To the modern mind this might seem a bit strange, because the authors make it obvious to the reader,
who share this common background knowledge, that they are making stuff up to please others.
However, the political usage of Act is more important than the historical accuracy.
These people are trying to defend themselves and find a place within their society where they can live and prosper.

This contradicts the expectations that these Early Christians are evangelizing, because in a way, they are doing the opposite.
The reason is that being a vulnerable minority, these people are trying to build bridges,
while still insisting on their own beliefs, because they have families and friends that can not easily convert to another religion all at once.
The social network they have is a momentum that meets obstacles when facing persecution,
but they are allowed to lie on behalf of their own group.
This was a sign of loyalty and not treason against their faith.

Unlike the story of Peter, that lies about being a follower of Jesus when pressured,
these people are honest about their faith,
but they are lying about the historical events they write about on behalf on their own group
and the author(s) of the text is kept anonymous, to protect themselves.
The problem is that while the author(s) might themselves be willing to die for their beliefs,
they have family and friends who might die as a consequence of their actions.

The eruption of Mount Vesuvius triggered this sort of debate where people are constructing false historical narratives,
to position themselves better in society and fit in, not primarily because they have an instrinsic need to spread their faith.
Later, when more people convert to Early Christianity, they might redacted texts to make it seem like spreading the gospel was their main purpose.

### 2. The Plague of Galen

In the period 165-180 CE, a devestating plague killed 5-10 million people in the Roman empire.
This was a lot of people at the time.

The Roman empire was at its greatest extent during the reign of Trajan, around 117 CE.
Its population peaked just before the Plague of Galen, also called "Antonine Plague".
There could have been about 75-76 million people at the time.

Plagues spreads more quickly when there are lots of people with highly connected social networks.
So, naturally, the Roman empire in this period gave ideal conditions to a naturally mutating virus.

At the time, people could not understand the plague, because bacteria was not yet discovered
and even if they had, they would probably not have done the experiment of filtering cow urine to discover viruses.
People simply did not have sufficient knowledge of how nature works.

However, this does not mean that people are stupid.
They see the potential in medicine and due to urgent needs, they produce stories dreaming about medicine in the future.
Since medicine is usually connected to religious rituals, by using entheogens in their drinks,
this ancient sci-fi fantasy genre about medicine gets tied up with savior figures.

The interest for medicine and healing miracles goes up drastically.
While the drug traffickers can not deliver on this high expectation by people,
they try to "spice up" their mythical narratives to sell more drugs.

It is a side effect of the market is there, for something that is not possible to produce,
but it also produces more attention toward specific products that are being sold.
With other words, more of the same. 
When more people buy these products,
it gets a viral effect, that in turn tempts more people to try new things,
generating more customers recursively.
What we observe in this period is that the spread of Early Christianity gets a boost and for some period grows exponentially.

Later, Christians told a story about Justin Martyr about this period, where he being executed, hence the nick name "Martyr".
Many scholars like to use this as a turning point when Early Christianity starts to spread significantly.
However, the Plague of Galen begins right at the same moment, so which is it: The plague, or the death of Justin Martyr?

In the same way that eruption of Mount Vesuvius was a major topic in the book of Acts,
the historical context is more likely to be a driving factor than the death of some outspoken person.
The combination of the two, that people payed more attention to these texts,
at the same time something extra-ordinary happened in history,
explains the observation selection effect,
where we end up with the same texts as part of our own historical myth.

Simply put:

    Plague of Galen => people paying more attention to Justin Martyr => texts by Justin Martyr becoming our distorted lens of history

This explanation is much simpler than Justin Martyr presenting something new and extra-ordinary with help from super-natural events.

### 3. Galen's public experiments to demonstrate localizing of Plato's tripartite soul

When people today think about about the 2nd century and read Early Christian texts,
it might seem as if those people writing these texts are just debating about theological points,
as if this was something people just did as part of their religious tradition.
However, this mental model underestimates the historical context of this particular time period and the motivation people have for producing such texts.

Plato had, centuries earlier, previously suggested as tripartite model of the soul,
where one part is associated with the head,
a second part is associated with the heart
and a third part is associated with the stomach.

To people living in this time, Plato's model would seem like an ancient curiosity,
while they stuck to their own simplified cultural model of one soul, one spirit and one body.
What people are taught by their parents rarely change over time, unless people have to change.
So, there is a kind of tripartite model of life already, but it does not make falsifiable claims about nature.
People tend to like unfalsifiable models if they are allowed to choose between one falsifiable model and one unfalsifiable model,
because in the unfalsifiable model, nobody can prove them wrong.
Plato's tripartitle model is too weird for people's taste precisely because it suggests something particular,
like something you could potentially observe directly,
which would be weird for people because while they have stories about the divine,
these things are never observed directly by anyone, ever.
They would not be comfortable with Plato's model, because it encourages questioning.

This changes when Galen performs vivisections on pigs in public.

Basically, he cut off nerves while doing open surgery on living pigs,
to demonstrate what happens when these nerves are not functioning.
The people observing these experiments had generations of experience with pigs,
so they would be able to infer a lot of data, enough to convince them to change their beliefs.

While the weakness of Plato's tripartite model of the soul was that is was falsifiable (in the eyes of ancient people),
it also produced an opportunity to perform an experiment that seems to people living at the time
as confirming the correctness of the model.

In a modern lens, we would not as easily be convinced by such experiments,
both because most modern people have less experiences with pigs,
but also that the modern mind views the soul as something deeper and more profound than in the ancient world.
With other words, we have moved the goalpost such that when the ancient mystery of the soul was solved in modern medicine,
we changed the definition of the problem such that it remains a mystery.

Ofc, this is a natural reaction to the scientific method,
but in the ancient world, people who start questioning their model of the soul was revolutionary.

Many Early Christian texts might be read through the lens that these people are writing about something,
in order to try unify their own cultural tradition with something new that they learned by rumor or observation.
They try to bring multiple perspectives together to reduce their cognitive dissonance.
The goal is to make them feel better about themselves, because uncertainty produces stress for the nervous system.

However, when people think they have figured out a solution,
they also discover that there are other people with others solutions that seem incompatible with their own.
This leads to heavy discussions about which doctrine is the correct one.
Since questioning existing beliefs is not a common part of the cultural tradition,
but more like intellectual engagement in dialogue,
they try to settle the dispute using rethoric and arguments.

Ofc, this does not work, so, centuries later, they start to kill each other over minor disagreements in beliefs about the soul.

### 4. The ethics of a good life in the context of fertility and prosperity in sense of Garden of Eden and the King Midas myth

The Garden of Eden is a story that most people today associate with the fall of humanity from an earlier more innocent state.
Yet at the same time, the Garden of Eden becomes a symbol of a good life, which is unreachable in some sense.
The idea is that what prevents people from living a good life is because they sin and somebody has to pay for this mistake.

In the modern lens, the Garden of Eden seems more naive, because we have more knowledge and see the world in an more analytic way.
However, to many ordinary poor people living in the ancient world, the Garden of Eden is basically synonymous with Paradise.
Many people picture their Utopia as having enough food and do nothing but eat and sleep.

The myths about gardens are in some sense to ordinary poor people in the ancient world their personal Paradise.
They might think about it when somebody asks them to imagine what a good life is.
Therefore, the deities of fertility and prosperity are enormously popular.

It is in this sense, of viewing gardens as Utopia or Paradise, that the myth of King Midas evolves.
Most people today do not think much about the garden in this myth.
This garden belongs to Zoe (Life), which is the king's daughter.

One can see how the play on gardens as symbolizing a good life, with the name Zoe,
which becomes the Tree of Life (Zoe's tree) in the Garden of Eden.

When people tell these stories, they often use a symbol inside the garden that represents the garden itself.
However, a story does not spread without a problem to be solved,
so this is where one gets King Midas golden touch, or the Tree of Knowledge, to bring problems into Paradise.

While most people today recognize the problem, they often ignore the very essence that this problem is built on.
Zoe (Life) is the manifestation and symbol of a good life itself and the mental model attributed to gardens as a way to achieve it.

It might seem a bit simplistic, but that is why ancient people are obsessed with gardens and luxury attached to it.
We tend to over-think what these people see in this stories and think about it more from a moral abstract perspective.
People at the time would simply think about the physical properties of gardens and how to maintain them.
The philosophical dimension about it would be regarded as too complex and getting a way of a good life,
hence this is symbolized through the Tree of Knowledge, that they thought was the root of all evil.

When we today say "Get a life", ancient people would say "Get a garden".

This influences the mental landscape these people navigate, so art and theatre is associated with maintaining a cultural garden.

Ancient people are far from intellectually incurious or looking down on knowledge in general.
This is a common misconception due to the modern myth we constructed around the Garden of Eden,
as a reaction in religion to the scientific revolution.
Early Christians just lived in a world that was poor on information.

### 5. Optimism bias and child indoctrination - how people perceived history through their own mystery religion as prophetizing their savior figure

Optimism bias can not be countered with any known method, except externalize reasoning to impartial systems.
This bias is essentially the belief that they personally have better access to information than other people.

For example, in The Century of Satire project, one goal is to apply Joker Calculus as a method to study language biases in Early Christian texts.
The formal language itself might seem naive, but it provides a mental anchor to counter-act some human biases.

The human brain can not hold many independent facts at the same time in working memory.
This means, we are particularly ill suited to understand and analyze complex networks of information such as history.
One way to deal with this is to develop information systems, like hyperlinks between texts,
to organize large amounts of information in a way that can be digested by the human brain.

When people transmit knowledge from one generation to another, there is an additional bias in social and political environments.
This means, that the noise to signal ratio in the information being taught is very low, far from theoretical optimal.

You can imagine how bad it was in the ancient world.

Regardless of mystery religion, all sects claimed that their particular savior figure was the one that was coming to save the world.
There thousands of texts written by people that claim with outmost confidence, like Paul, to have direct spiritual revelations.
However, these texts are contradictory to each other and they often engage in heated discussions about which belief is correct.

When children are being brought up in some mystery religion, they are simultaneously taught to be arrogant and confident in their own beliefs,
but this also creates ideological fronts of war with other religions, where bad training in history produces very weird results.
This can lead to an effect where people miss out that many ideas they have come from Plato and Aristotle, that disagreed and debates among themselves.

The fact this is actually a debate spanning centuries that stupid people turn into war, is a tragic accident of history.
Because, if people knew, then they could spend their energy on more fruitful projects.

### 6. Cross-cultural criticism, reputation and living hood - how intellectuals survived in the ancient world

Early Christians, despite many of them having the same ethnic origin as Jews,
because these two religions were not split at the time,
have to engage with the broader Hellenistic culture in their texts.
They not primary concerned with internal conflicts within their own culture,
but the outlook on the world.
The goal is to reach cosmic harmony and unifying all kinds of knowledge accessible to mankind.

This is because people are not so stupid that they believe they can just ignore the world.
Yet, at the same time, witnessing and learning about horrible events that happen,
it is understandable that the impression of the world in general is not very high.
When people grow up in a religion, they are often not told important things about historical events that puts their own beliefs into doubts.
As a consequence, when interacting with people that have other believes,
people tend to behave as if their own culture has never done anything wrong.

Due to this process, people tend to label critics as haters of their own religion,
while the critics might think of themselves as just pointing out flaws that practitioners of that religion do not see or reflect upon.

It is easy to divide people using labels and this is sufficient sometimes to produce violent conflicts.

When an intellectual is attacked, this might be perceived as not just an attack on their own work,
but an attack on them personally and their family, with their ethnic and cultural background in general.
So, people see themselves as heroes defending their tribe from the enemy.
The effect of this is that when the pen meets the paper, they go to war, verbally speaking.

There is no end to condemnation of other people's views, where demons and angels are summoned in a battle of literacy.
The idea is to impress other people, but also win arguments that addresses the core of their own personal political identity.

Without this process, the intellectual could not receive high reputation.
To make living, they had to fight with words, or else they might be seen as cowardly.
There are some people who are more peaceful than others,
e.g. Eratosthenes, which achieves magnificent breakthroughs,
yet receive heavy criticism because they seem to others like easy targets.
This tendency has kept knowledge back for centuries.

However, at the same time, intellectual are engaging across cultures,
addressing and arguing based on common knowledge that spans the interests of people living within a specific culture.
To understand Early Christian texts, it is important that this common background spanning multiple perspectives,
with shared philosophical roots that are not tied to specific religions,
is contributing significantly to the discussions that people are having.

When this common knowledge is lost, history enters a darker period,
because the information networks people have access to are less functioning.
For example, not all texts by Aristotle are known in Western Europe for centuries,
despite that scholars in the Byzantine empire know about them.
When the Byzantine empire falls, some scholars escape with these texts that creates an intellectual shock in Western universities.

This was how the scientific revolution started.
