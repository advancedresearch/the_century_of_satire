# The Ouroboros Hypothesis

To reconstruct 2nd century cultural literature context of Roman satire in Early Christian texts,
it is useful to consider a hypothesis that the literary Jesus (Christ) originated as a story for children written in Greek,
in the genre of Roman satire.

When studying the origin of Early Christianity scientifically, it is common to distinguish between two versions of Jesus in two senses:

- As a historical person
- As a literary character

For example in modern times, when studying the origin of Santa Claus,
it is very bad scholarship to mix the historial person Nicholas of Myra
with the character Santa Claus based on this person.

Why? Because Santa Claus in a cultural literature context has very little in common with Nicholas of Myra.

By common cultural tropes, Santa Claus lives on the North Pole and travels around the world at Christmas Eve to deliver presents to children.

If you treat the "real history" of the Santa Claus character as the average between Nicholas of Myra and the commonly portrayed Santa Claus,
you will not get a hypothesis that explains both how Nicholas of Myra inspired the stories about Santa Claus
and how the Santa Claus character developed over time.

Instead, the average between these two versions, history and literature, is worse than either one set apart from each other.

This is what The Ouroboros Hypothesis explains:

- How Early Christians started to use a Jewish Messiah figure (a possible historical Jesus) to create their stories
- How Roman satire is used across all the Early Christian texts, many that were written by different people in different places

This project, "The Century of Satire" project, aims at reconstructing 2nd century cultural literature context of Roman satire in Early Christian texts.
It was the first project that suggested The Ouroboros Hypothesis, which is now gaining some popularity among biblical scholars.

However, when The Ouroboros Hypothesis was first formulated, it was merely part of exploring one violation of [historical assumptions for chronology](./formal-rules-for-historical-intuition.md#formal-rules-important-for-chronology).

In religious mythology, all kinds of historical assumptions for chronology get violated.
For example, the character Melchizedek in Jewish mythology does not get born or die,
but exists beyond a normal lifespan that is expected for any historical person.

Now, seen through the lens of ancient people, what makes the Melchizedek character so great?
There is not much substantial content produced by writers about the properties this character has otherwise.
Melchizedek does not do very much, similar to commonly mythology around individual angels.

Here, it is very important to not fall into the trap of a particular modern mindset when interpreting ancient religions.
The people who practised ancient religion did not ask critical questions about characters themselves.
Instead, when some character is introduced into mythology, it is meant for people to use their imagination.
People argued with each other what their own imagination had come up with and what they had heard from their own friends.
Therefore, when people argue about characters, this is not about characters themselves, but about the people who imagine stuff about them.
Criticism and critical thinking about ancient religious characters was invariably seen as a personal attack,
against some individual or a group of people.

It is also important to keep in mind that ancient religion is not just about literature.
In production of religious music, people do not in general have the same language biases as when they write texts.
There is no "wrong" way to produce religious music, because music is seen as a community effort.
Here, everybody are welcome to participate and strengthen the community.

Literature in ancient religion is mostly a fight for dominance and authority.
This takes place alongside other religious traditions.
It is common for modern people to think only about literature and conflicts,
without actually seeing the greater picture of how ancient religion worked.

An angel in mythology does not need much of a backstory.
Simply being an angel alone, was enough for ancient people to be creative about how the angel looked like or what role it played.
After all, if you imagine that angels were seen as invisible physical beings, not just something people believed in,
from an individual perspective who grows up in some religious tradition,
some angels might feel very real to them and belived to behave in particular ways, as if the person who believes is knowing and seeing these angels.

Therefore, a character like Melchizedek functions more like an imaginary friend,
which a person can use their own creativity and imagination,
but in a social accepted way since this character has been introduced through the proper religious authority.
Due to this imaginary relationship, when Jesus later comes along,
the author of the book of Hebrew, which likes Melchizedek, equates Jesus to Melchizedek so that they can continue this imaginary relationship
under social acceptable circumstances where the norms are changing from Melchizedek to Jesus.

Most biblical scholars today believe that the Melchizedek character was made up based on a psalm,
which got its own backstory and inserted into the story of Abraham.

Therefore, if you try to think of the Melchizedek character as historical, you are missing what is going on.

Why do you believe that inserting a new text into the story of Abraham was acceptable at the time?

This happens because first, some person uses their creativity and imagination,
until Melchizedek feels real to them, until somebody, who also likes Melchizedek, has the authority to modify the text,
without anyone else being able to stop them.
By inserting this new text into the story of Abraham, this shows the important status of the person who did it.
Changing existing literature is a social risk, that invites criticism, but is mostly done to expand authority and influence.

With other words, historical accuracy is irrelevant.
Furthermore, the genre of literature is also irrelevant.
It does not matter whether some text is prophetic, a heroic story or Roman satire.
Across all these genres, people argue about which version of their imaginative savior figure should be prioritized socially.

Literature in the ancient world is seen as a way to influence the world.
As religious authority is a scarce resource, people fight over the control of it.

The Ouroboros Hypothesis takes into account all aspects of ancient religion and the scientific evidence we have about Early Christianity.
It explains how Christianity first begins, not as mainly a historical event, but as a literary event.
Some person produces a text in Greek, not just to gain influence socially, but also to impress others with their skills.
Hence, the historical person and the literary character is better explained separately, because mixing them up does not help understanding either one of them.

This literary event can be broken into a set of 3 propositions:

1. A story told in book format
2. The main character "climbs out" of the last page of the book and jumps into the first page
3. A literary commentary on Roman translation fables, with death, missing body, witness and ascension to heaven

It does not actually matter for this hypothesis which character that first inspired the Early Christians.

A such literary event is scientifically falsifiable.
It has to happen under particular circumstances in language technology,
in a period where people are arguing about benefits and tradeoffs between books and scrolls.
Therefore, it can not happen in any time period.
Certainly not in cultures where book formats are overwhelmingly dominant such as the modern world, 
or in cultures where scrolls or clay tablets are overwhelmingly dominant.

The only known time period and place that The Ouroboros Hypothesis is applicable historically, is to late 1st to 2nd century Early Christianity.

If The Ouroboros Hypothesis is false, then it almost certainly never happens historically.
However, if the hypothesis is true, then it shaped a whole era of literature and religion,
even most people were unaware about this particular literary event in their own past.

The Ouroboros Hypothesis does not explain a "true Christianity", like many attempts to find the origin of Christianity.
Instead, it explains a particular literary mechanism that triggered people living in the 2nd century to write thousands of Early Christian texts,
that vary greatly in content and religious beliefs.

In addition to the set of 3 propositions above, The Ouroboros Hypothesis explains calendar obsession among Early Christians.
The calendar of modern times starts at the commonly believed birth of Jesus and has 12 months per year.
During this period, the official temple practice of Jerusalem followed a calendar with 13 months per year.

If The Ouroboros Hypothesis is correct, then the original text which produced this literary event,
was used as a liturgical text on a yearly basis, such that when the main character "climbs out" of the last page
and jumps into the first page, marks the start of a new year.

Specifically, this mark of a new year, would likely be at Easter.

This is a scientific prediction about the use of the original text, which can be tested against new and existing evidence.

The name "Ouroboros" comes from the symbol of a snake that bites its own tail, which means a cyclic yearly calendar.
This is the basic meaning, upon which people construct additional layers of cultural complexity.

With other words, The Ouroboros Hypothesis does not restrict how Early Christians interpret or believe their texts,
except that they are building on a similar foundational layer that originates with this literary event.
Technically, this literary event can only happen around the time when people transition from 13 months to 12 months in their calendar.

There is scientific evidence that when people transition from 13 months to 12 months in this time period and place,
it coincides with the commonly held belief of the origin of Christianity.

Therefore, viewing the origin of Christianity as some important historical event that caused people to change their calendar,
is an entirely wrong perspective.

Instead, the origin of Christianity should be thought of as a literary event,
where a new religion post-historically claims authority over the transition to a new calendar, from 13 to 12 months specifically.

Therefore, The Ouroboros Hypothesis is about a literary event,
when people started to view a new calendar through the lens of their own religion.
This started before people had produced all their detailed beliefs about this period in history.
Mainly, a literary event where many of the details that people took for granted later, are missing.
This literary event is built upon a story for children, written in Greek, in the genre of Roman satire.

With other words, the original text would probably not have been serious in tone,
but as a tongue-in-cheek humor,
explained the new calendar in a way that people found funny,
given the contemporary cultural context at the time.

Christianity originates as a new religion, when people first start to take this original text seriously.

Now, this transition into a serious religion, does not happen overnight.
It happens gradually, over decades and centuries.
There are still many features that trace back to its beginning, even today,
of the satirical debates going on in the 2nd century among Early Christians.
The cultural gap between modern religion, which is detached from the practice of medicine,
and ancient religion which was primarily about integrating compatibles practice of medicine, politics and economics,
makes it difficult for modern people to understand how Early Christians both were joking around,
but also took their debates deadly seriously.

Roman satire is not like modern satire, which is non-violent.

In Roman satire, the goal is to out-wit opponents, with the aim to dominate them culturally and socially.
Very frequently, people had fights and sometimes killed each other over satirical arguments.

The desire to kill each other can be read through the lines in Early Christian texts.
Authors use their own savior figure to take the lives of other savior figures.
This is not meant as historical accurate events, but they are often presented as historical to convert followers to their own side.

What we today think of as lying and making up stuff, was actually the entire point in Roman satire.

Roman satire literature is used to assert dominance, to provoke others to attack and to defend against accusations.

So, when modern people read these texts with the idea in mind that they are supposed to be historically accurate,
they are missing the entire reason why people produced these sorts of texts in the first place:

- Lying in Roman satire is not something that people hide
- Lying in Roman satire is the point

When somebody accuses an author of lying, when the author writes in the genre of Roman satire,
then how do people typically respond?

They respond by pretending they are serious and accuse the attackers of lying, to ridicule them.

In turn, the attacker gets offended and launches new attacks.

This process continues the entire 2nd century and into the 3rd century.
Early Christians are obsessed with their culture wars and do not prioritize recording historical events.
These people are so obsessed with quarrels, that there is no record of any ordinary poor Early Christian name, the entire 2nd century.

Despite that we have thousands of Early Christian texts from this period,
there is basically no scientific data about who these people were and how their social lives were organized.
This holds across multiple languages and nations.
Seemingly, all their surplus energy went to launching attacks on others and defending themselves from criticism.

The accusations of the surviving Orthodox texts against other sects,
were probably met with similar rhetoric in response.

The best way to think about Early Christians during the 2nd century,
is as a large cultural battlefield,
where people are trying to outsmart each other,
starting quarrels, lying and making stuff up,
accusing others of not speaking the truth or to be historically inaccurate,
while all this hypocrisy was seen as social acceptable,
simply due to worshiping their own savior figure.

This has basically nothing to do with historical events.
Early Christians might project their own creativity and imagination upon such historical events,
in a very toxic way, where only particular events and particular characters matter,
because these were the points that people were arguing about.

The Ouroboros Hypothesis explains the origin of Christianity as a literary event,
one that invites people to use their own creativity and imagination,
to fuel heated debates over which imaginary version is the correct one,
as a way to assert authority and dominance over others.

However, the literary side is just on aspect.
The very next morning, after a drunken evening with a bar fight,
people might sing together hymns to the planet Venus,
before they gathered around a meal.
